Thursday, November 26, 2026

Newsletter Summary

The S&P 500 slipped 0.50% to 7,201.09 and the Nasdaq fell 0.62% to 23,585.80, while the Dow added 0.33% to 50,182.95, a split that says more about index composition than about broad risk appetite. Gold added 0.61% to 327.45 but remains near a 15-month low as rate-hike expectations continue to weigh on the metal. Underneath the index moves, leveraged finance has its own thread running: JPMorgan is flagging a $450 billion wave of AI-related bond issuance even as credit stays skeptical of the equity bounce.

Macro Update

December Hike Now Priced as Near-Certainty

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge climbed, the Beige Book showed most regional boards wanted to raise rates back in October, and TradingView cites hike odds at 98% heading into the December meeting. That compressed uncertainty is why SOFR firmed to 3.94% even as the 10-year eased to 4.91%, a curve move that reflects policy path resolution more than growth optimism. South Korea's own hike to a two-year high reinforces that this is a synchronized, not isolated, tightening impulse.

Read more

ABC News reported the Fed's preferred inflation gauge climbing ahead of a possible rate hike, while Bloomberg's reporting on the Beige Book noted most Fed regional boards wanted to raise rates in October. TradingView cited hike odds at 98% alongside a contrarian warning from Robin Brooks of a potential 'full meltdown.' In the data, SOFR rose to 3.94% (+0.51%), the 2-year held flat at 4.63%, and the 10-year eased to 4.91% (-0.20%).

The mechanism here is a resolved data-dependency: once the Beige Book anecdotes and the PCE print removed ambiguity about December, the front end simply holds at the new expected level while the long end eases because a hike this cycle, paired with tariff-driven growth risk, caps the term premium rather than lifting it. SOFR moves first and most mechanically because policy transmission to overnight funding is immediate, while the 10-year is a function of growth and term-premium expectations that are now pulling the other way.

For leveraged finance desks the transmission is direct: floating-rate loan coupons reset off SOFR, so a firming benchmark flows straight through to interest coverage on floating-rate capital structures. That is part of why HY OAS crept up to 3.72% and IG OAS to 0.98%, spreads pricing marginally higher carrying costs for issuers even as absolute levels remain tight by historical standards rather than signaling credit stress.

Watch the actual December FOMC statement for whether the Beige Book's regional anecdotes translate into unanimous committee language, and watch whether South Korea's hike to 3.25% proves to be the first of a broader wave of non-US tightening that would reinforce the global rate-pressure read rather than a one-off.

Sources: ABC News · Bloomberg.com · TradingView

Tariff Headlines Snap the AI Rally's Momentum

Reuters and Korea Times both ran the same line today, that world stocks are falling as Trump tariff plans rattle markets and the AI rally fades, and the index split backs it up: Nasdaq -0.62% underperformed the Dow's +0.33%, Eurostoxx fell 0.48% and Shanghai fell 0.61%, while the Nikkei rose 0.86% and the FTSE rose 0.55%. This is not a uniform selloff so much as a rotation away from the tariff-exposed, AI-levered growth trade.

Read more

The headline flow is consistent across outlets: Reuters and Korea Times both describe world stocks tumbling as new Trump tariffs are floated and the AI rally fades, with a separate Reuters piece comparing proposed India tariffs against rival nations. The market reaction was uneven rather than broad-based: Nasdaq -0.62%, S&P -0.50%, Eurostoxx -0.48%, and Shanghai -0.61% all fell, while the Dow +0.33%, Nikkei +0.86%, and FTSE +0.55% advanced, and WTI rose 1.01% to $82.24.

The mechanism is a tariff shock landing unevenly across sectors and geographies. Long-duration, AI-levered growth names are most exposed to the combination of tariff-driven input cost uncertainty and a multiple that was already stretched, which explains Nasdaq's underperformance relative to the Dow's more value-tilted composition. Oil's gain is consistent with tariff and supply-chain disruption premium rather than a demand story.

The read-through to financing conditions is that this has not yet become a credit event. The dollar was essentially flat (DXY +0.06%) rather than bid as a safe haven, gold rose only 0.61% and remains near a 15-month low, and HY OAS and IG OAS both moved only a handful of basis points. If this were escalating into a genuine trade-war stress scenario, you would expect a sharper dollar or gold bid and a faster widening in spreads than what printed today.

Watch the specifics of the actual tariff announcements, particularly how the India comparison piece frames implementation timing, and watch whether Nasdaq's underperformance versus the Dow persists or reverses once the initial headline reaction fades.

Sources: Reuters · Investment Week · Reuters

The AI Debt Wave Credit Desks Are Bracing For

JPMorgan is warning of a $450 billion wave of AI-related bond issuance, and the Moomoo recap of the Dow's recent 'deep V' session notes the credit market remains unconvinced by equity resilience. Separately, banks are pitching CVC €11 billion of debt for a possible Verisure deal, confirming that jumbo LBO underwriting is still moving forward into this backdrop. Spreads have only drifted a handful of basis points so far, but the supply pipeline is the thing to watch.

Read more

JPMorgan's $450 billion estimate for prospective AI-related bond issuance was cited alongside the observation that the Dow's bounce has not fully carried the credit market. Separately, Bloomberg reported banks pitching CVC €11 billion of debt for a possible Verisure transaction. In the spread data, HY OAS rose to 3.72% (+2.48%, roughly 9bps) and IG OAS to 0.98% (+1.03%, about 1bp), while HYG was essentially flat at 76.52 (+0.12%).

The mechanism linking AI capex to credit spreads is a supply story more than a quality story. Hyperscalers and AI infrastructure buildouts are increasingly funded with debt rather than free cash flow, concentrating a large, correlated block of new issuance into a market that is simultaneously absorbing a higher-for-longer policy rate. Incremental supply of that scale widens spreads at the margin independent of any change in underlying credit fundamentals.

For leveraged finance specifically, this matters because large LBO packages like the Verisure €11 billion financing have to clear into the same investor base that is being asked to absorb the AI bond wave, competing for the same floating-rate and high-yield allocations. Artisan's Q3 commentary on its floating-rate fund suggests managers are already positioning around that supply dynamic rather than being caught by it.

Watch the actual pricing and order book on the Verisure financing as a live read on demand, and watch whether the pace of AI-related issuance materializes near JPMorgan's $450 billion figure. If that supply clears without meaningful spread concession, today's modest 9bp HY drift stays a non-event, if it does not, HY OAS has room to move further from here.

Sources: Moomoo · Finimize · Bloomberg.com

The move is a straight supply-risk repricing. Reuters reported Houthi forces claiming an attack on a Saudi refinery, and EnergyNow separately flagged that crude is extending gains as hopes fade for a deal that markets had been pricing as a near-term lid on prices. A third EnergyNow piece warns that global oil inventories are already depleted, raising the stakes of any further supply shock. None of this is quantified in barrels or dollars in the headlines, but directionally it is enough to push WTI up a full point on the session.

The record highs are concentrated in producers with direct crude leverage rather than the integrateds or refiners, consistent with a headline flagging three oil stocks explicitly tied to higher crude prices. Canadian energy equities are cited as eyeing their first record since 2008, which tells you the move is broad across North American upstream rather than a single-name story. A refinery attack is normally a product-crack story more than a crude story, so the fact that WTI itself is leading suggests the market is trading the broader supply-disruption narrative, not just the one incident.

Higher realized crude prices are a direct tailwind for E&P free cash flow and credit profiles, which matters given HY OAS widened 2.48% and IG OAS widened 1.03% on the session, both modest moves but a reminder that credit is drifting wider even as equities for commodity-linked issuers rally. For leveraged upstream names, stronger near-term cash generation reduces refinancing risk and can support earlier-than-planned debt paydown, a dynamic that partially offsets the broader spread widening for energy-specific paper.

The setup from here hinges on confirmation. Houthi claims of attacks have not always translated into verified damage or sustained output loss, so the next catalyst is whether Saudi officials or satellite evidence corroborate the refinery hit. Equally, any sign that the broader deal talks referenced by EnergyNow are back on track would remove the fading-hopes bid under crude just as quickly as it arrived, which is the main downside risk to today's energy outperformance.

Read more

The sector's modest outperformance today is a straightforward low-beta story rather than a company-specific one.
