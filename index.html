Macro Update

CoreWeave Rout Drags Nasdaq, Freezes Data Center Debt

The Nasdaq fell 2.60% to 26,008.86 even as the S&P and Dow held up, with CoreWeave's widening bond yields and sinking stock the identifiable driver. International Financing Review reports the move has frozen data center financing conversations, which matters because a chunk of the AI capex buildout Forbes pegs at half a trillion dollars runs through credit exactly like this.

Read more

CoreWeave's stock sank and its bond yields spiked on the session, according to CNBC, and IFR reports that the data center financing market has effectively frozen in response. That single-name stress is enough to explain most of the 2.60% gap between the Nasdaq's decline and the S&P's 0.58% gain, since the index's AI-infrastructure-linked names carry disproportionate weight.

The transmission mechanism runs through comparables. When one AI-infra credit's bond yields spike, underwriters mark similar paper wider across the sector, and the warehouse facilities and private credit vehicles backing data center buildouts get repriced at the margin. Higher implied cost of capital compresses the equity multiple on every name tied to the same capex cycle, which is why the damage shows up concentrated in the Nasdaq rather than the broader tape.

Forbes frames the AI debt boom at half a trillion dollars, much of it financed outside traditional high yield through private credit and structured vehicles, which means the sector-wide stress here may not fully show up in the HY OAS print of 3.27 even though that index tightened on the day. Mexico Business News separately notes the IPO pipeline is weakening on macro volatility and tech valuation concerns, consistent with financing conditions tightening for growth-stage and capex-heavy issuers even as headline credit spreads look calm.

Watch whether the CoreWeave-linked spread widening stays contained to that name and its direct comparables or whether it bleeds into the broader HY OAS print over coming sessions. A reopening of data center financing at only modestly wider terms would support the single-name read; continued freezing would suggest the AI capex financing chain itself is the story, not just one credit.

Sources: International Financing Review · CNBC · Forbes

Fed Holds, Data Won't Cooperate

The Fed held rates steady in its first move since the Iran war spiked oil prices, in what Bloomberg calls the most confusing economy the Fed has faced, with unemployment up 2.44% to 4.20% even as weekly jobless claims sit near 57-year lows. CPI is running 3.72% year over year with the index up 0.65% in the latest read, and the 10-year at 5.13% and 2-year at 4.81% both rose on the day as Treasury yields priced in fewer near-term cuts.

Read more

The unemployment rate rose 2.44% to 4.20% on the day's data, yet weekly jobless claims remain near 57-year lows at levels CNBC and Reuters both describe as consistent with a low-fire trend, with the latest print at 194,000 against a 200,000 estimate. CPI is running 3.72% year over year with the index at 336.30, up 0.65%. The 10-year yield rose to 5.13%, the 2-year to 4.81%, and SOFR to 3.90%, all moving higher on the session.

This is a low-fire, low-hire labor market rather than a layoff cycle, which denies the Fed a clean signal in either direction. Companies aren't shedding workers, which keeps claims low, but they also aren't hiring, which lets the unemployment rate drift up through slower labor force absorption. Treasury yields rose because the market is repricing the odds of near-term cuts lower given inflation still running above target, which is why the 10-year, 2-year, and SOFR all moved together.

Higher back-end yields raise the cost of carry for leveraged issuers refinancing into this market, and the move in SOFR to 3.90% feeds directly into floating-rate loan and CLO coupon resets, which is consistent with Seeking Alpha's note that CLOs outperformed as yields rose in the third quarter. The IG OAS widening 1.15% to 0.88 lines up with this too, since investment grade's longer duration profile absorbs more of a rate move than high yield does.

Watch the next jobless claims print for continuation of the low-fire pattern and the next CPI read for whether the 3.72% year-over-year rate starts to roll over. Futu's framing that Powell faces a tough challenge next week anchoring inflation expectations suggests the coming FOMC commentary, not just the rate decision itself, is the thing to watch for a read on how long the pause holds.

Sources: Bloomberg.com · ABC News · Reuters · CNBC

HY Tightens While IG Widens, an Unusual Split

High yield OAS tightened 1.80% to 3.27 even as investment grade OAS widened 1.15% to 0.88, inverting the typical pattern where IG outperforms HY when equities sell off. HYG itself barely moved, up 0.16% to 77.10, confirming this was a spread story rather than a price story, and it lines up with Seeking Alpha's note that CLOs outperformed as yields rose in the third quarter.

Read more

HY OAS fell 1.80% to 3.27 while IG OAS rose 1.15% to 0.88 on the same session that saw the Nasdaq drop 2.60%. HYG, the high yield ETF proxy, was essentially flat at 77.10, up just 0.16%, which confirms the spread move did not translate into a broad high yield price selloff.

The divergence traces to duration. IG's longer average duration makes it more sensitive to the day's rise in Treasury yields, with the 10-year at 5.13% and 2-year at 4.81% both up, so IG prices absorb more of a rate shock than HY does. HY's relative resilience also reflects a continued floating-rate bid, since SOFR rising to 3.90% resets CLO liability coupons higher and keeps the arbitrage behind CLO equity tranches working at current spread levels, consistent with Seeking Alpha's Q3 CLO outperformance thesis.

For primary calendars this matters because HY issuers can apparently still price inside levels that would normally tighten only in a risk-on tape, even with single-name stress sitting elsewhere in the market around CoreWeave, while IG issuers face a higher embedded cost of capital purely from the rate move. Real Investment Advice's note that small caps have recently traded like a bond proxy is worth flagging here too, though the Russell was essentially flat on the day at plus 0.07%, so that dynamic did not show up forcefully in today's print.

Watch whether HY OAS keeps compressing even as IG leaks wider, which would support the CLO-driven, floating-rate-bid explanation. A reversal, particularly if the CoreWeave-adjacent credit stress broadens into the broader HY universe, would move the 3.27 OAS quickly given how little room it has to tighten further from already-low levels.

Sources: Seeking Alpha · Real Investment Advice · Morningstar
