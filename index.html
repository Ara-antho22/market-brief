Macro Update

FOMC Hold Day: The Curve Already Knows the Answer

Every major outlet covering the Fed today, Reuters, the Guardian, the New York Times, ABC, AFP, and Fox, frames this as a steady hold, the last decision before midterms. The setup is a genuine cross-current: unemployment up 2.44% to 4.20% even as CPI holds at 3.72% year over year, leaving the Fed with no clean signal to act on. SOFR has already risen 0.51% to 3.95% and the 10-year is flat at 5.00%, while the 2-year has slipped 21 basis points to 4.68%, pricing some probability of cuts later even with no move today. The real story is less the decision itself than what the statement language does to a curve that has already made up its mind about direction.

Read more

Every headline on the Fed today lands on the same word: hold. Reuters calls it a move to wait-and-see mode, the Guardian and NYT frame it as the last decision before midterms, and WSJ notes global government bond yields rising into both the Fed decision and the UK budget. The data backs the holding pattern: unemployment rose 2.44% to 4.20%, CPI year over year sits at 3.72%, SOFR has climbed 0.51% to 3.95%, the 10-year is unchanged at 5.00%, and the 2-year has fallen 0.21% to 4.68%.

The mechanism here is that a rising unemployment rate and above-target inflation pull the Fed in opposite directions at once, so the path of least resistance is no path at all. That keeps the base rate, and therefore SOFR, anchored at a level that is restrictive relative to where the 2-year is already drifting, which is why the front end is flattening against a 10-year that WSJ notes is being pushed up globally by fiscal supply concerns tied to events like the UK budget rather than by Fed expectations alone.

For leveraged balance sheets, the relevant number is the SOFR print itself, since floating-rate loans and most leveraged credit facilities reprice off it directly. A SOFR at 3.95%, up half a point on the data, means carry costs on floating structures stay elevated regardless of what the Fed says in the statement, even as HY OAS has tightened to 3.30. That combination, high base cost but compressing risk premium, is the exact environment leveraged issuers are refinancing 2026 maturity walls into.

Watch the statement's wait-and-see language and whether the press conference gives any hint of a December move, since the 2-year's 21 basis point decline suggests the market is already leaning dovish for the next meeting rather than this one. Also watch whether the political framing multiple outlets are using, a hold explicitly timed around midterms, shows up as commentary from the Chair himself.

Sources: Reuters · WSJ

Spreads Tighten Into the Decision, But Not Everywhere

HY OAS tightened 2.37% to 3.30 and IG OAS fell 1.10% to 0.90, with HYG up 0.52% to 77.60, a risk-on move in credit heading into the Fed. The FT is flagging this explicitly as an interesting moment for low-quality paper, while CNBC reports Goldman has identified European corporate borrowers vulnerable to rising debt stress, a divergence that matters for anyone running cross-border capital structures.

Read more

HY OAS compressed 2.37% to 3.30 and IG OAS fell 1.10% to 0.90 on the session, with the HYG ETF up 0.52% to 77.60. The FT's framing of the moment for trashy credit lines up with product-level evidence of demand, including a new active USD high yield fund launch and continued commentary on CLO relative value. On the other side of the Atlantic, CNBC reports Goldman flagging specific European corporates as vulnerable to debt stress hitting corporate borrowing costs.

The mechanism is that spread compression happening alongside a flat 10-year at 5.00% and a rising SOFR at 3.95% means the risk premium embedded in credit is shrinking even though the base cost of capital is not. That is the signature of a demand-driven technical, investors reaching for yield through new fund launches and CLO structures, rather than a genuine improvement in the default outlook, since nothing in the underlying rate picture has eased.

For leveraged issuers, tighter spreads lower the marginal cost of a new deal even if SOFR itself stays high, which should support primary HY and leveraged loan calendars in the near term. But the Goldman-flagged European stress is a reminder that the compression is not global. Any capital structure with euro-denominated tranches or European subsidiary exposure is facing a different borrowing cost trajectory than the one implied by US HY OAS at 3.30, a bifurcation worth pricing into relative value work rather than assuming US spread tightness travels.

Watch whether HY OAS holds the 3.30 level through the Fed statement and press conference, since any hawkish surprise would test how much of the current tightness is technical versus fundamental. Also watch for follow-through from the UK budget, which WSJ ties directly to the move in global bond yields, for any read-across into European corporate spread levels Goldman has flagged as vulnerable.

Sources: Financial Times · CNBC

Internationally, the Nikkei added 1.08% and the Eurostoxx 0.94%, while the FTSE fell 0.88% and the Shanghai Composite slipped 0.52%. Bloomberg's coverage notes gold holding above $4,000 as traders look to the Fed decision, consistent with the data showing a 1.21% gold gain on the session.

A falling VIX heading into a well-telegraphed policy decision is the options market's way of confirming the event is priced as low-risk, which frees capital toward higher-beta names. That is why the Nasdaq and Russell are outperforming the large-cap-heavy Dow today, small caps and growth carry more duration and balance sheet sensitivity to the front end of the curve, and the front end, via the 2-year's 21 basis point decline, is already signaling a friendlier rate path than the long end.

The more interesting tension is in oil, down 1.93% to $107.89 even as Reuters flagged a Saudi Arabian refinery attack reigniting supply risk. If that disconnect holds, it suggests crude is trading more on demand expectations or inventory data than geopolitical headline risk right now, which is relevant to the Fed's inflation read this afternoon since cheaper crude flows directly into the CPI components the Fed cited in its own wait-and-see framing.

Watch whether the VIX stays below 19 through the 2pm statement or spikes on any language surprise, whether oil stabilizes or extends its decline if the Saudi refinery damage is confirmed as material, and whether Russell and Nasdaq outperformance survives the press conference given their higher sensitivity to any shift in front-end rate expectations.
