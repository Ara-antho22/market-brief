Morning Edition

Monday, November 23, 2026

Newsletter Summary

Equities sold off broadly, with the S&P 500 down 2.02% to 7,175.83, the Nasdaq off 2.25% to 23,879.20, and the Russell 2000 lagging at -2.88%. The move was global and uneven in severity: Japan's Nikkei dropped 5.06% to 56,436.65, by far the day's sharpest move, while the Eurostoxx 50 fell 2.86% and the FTSE and Shanghai were down a more modest 1.58% and 0.95%. The VIX jumped 15.8% to 28.44, a real move by its own standards, yet credit spreads barely budged, with HY OAS up just 1.44% to 3.52 and HYG down only 0.26%. The 10-year yield actually eased to 5.04% even as Fed minutes and commentary point toward hike risk rather than cuts, and the dollar index held essentially flat at 122.65. Gold fell 3.67% to 322.87, extending a corrective stretch rather than acting as the haven a selloff like this would normally produce. The split between equity stress and credit calm is the thing to carry into this week's Fed decision.

Macro Update

Fed Hike Bets Reassert, Equities Take the Hit

Fed minutes and a run of commentary are pointing to hike risk rather than cuts, with the Conference Board citing a robust employment backdrop as support for further tightening. That repricing is landing on equities, not credit: the S&P fell 2.02% and the VIX jumped to 28.44, while HY OAS only widened to 3.52. The Fed decision this week lands on the same session as Broadcom earnings and a Micron deadline, concentrating the catalyst.

Read more

Fed minutes flagged hike risk and FXEmpire noted December hike bets are supporting the dollar, while the Conference Board argued the robust employment report supports further rate hikes. Jobless claims rose to 203,000 but layoffs remain low, and the unemployment rate sits at 4.10%, down 2.38% on the month. Against that backdrop the S&P 500 fell 2.02%, the Nasdaq 2.25%, and the VIX jumped 15.8% to 28.44.

The mechanism is a discount-rate story more than a credit story. Hike bets raise the risk-free rate used to value long-duration equity cash flows, hitting high-multiple tech and AI names hardest, which is consistent with the Nasdaq underperforming the Dow. Both ends of the curve stayed elevated, with 2s at 4.76% and 10s at 5.04%, and Wall Street is reportedly positioning for curve flattening, meaning the market is pricing more near-term tightening relative to long-run growth, which compresses equity multiples more than it directly raises corporate financing costs.

The financing read-through is in SOFR, at 3.92% and rising 0.26%, which flows straight into floating-rate leveraged loan debt service if hike bets persist. But HY OAS widened only to 3.52 and HYG slipped just 0.26%, meaning credit has not repriced for recession risk even as equity volatility spiked. That divergence between equity stress and credit calm is unstable and typically resolves in one direction within a week or two.

The explicit catalyst is this week's Fed decision, which converges with Broadcom earnings and a Micron deadline in a single session. Watch whether SOFR and the 2-year continue climbing after the decision, and whether HY OAS follows equities wider or holds near current levels, which would confirm this is a multiple and volatility event rather than the start of a credit event.

Sources: FXEmpire · The Conference Board · Crypto Briefing · Traders Union

Buyout Debt Cracks at the Margin as AI Capex Keeps Borrowing

JPMorgan is weighing selling $2 billion of software debt at 80 cents on the dollar, a direct sign of buyout financing pressure in leveraged credit even while index-level HY spreads stay contained. Separately, Goldman's SpaceX pitch is being used to help solve what's being called the AI debt puzzle, and investors are increasingly worried about Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft and Oracle borrowing to fund AI capex. This is where credit stress is actually showing up today, not in the broad OAS numbers.

Read more

JPMorgan is reportedly weighing a sale of $2 billion of software debt at 80 cents on the dollar as buyout financing pressure mounts, according to reporting on the situation. Separately, investor concern is building around AI-related borrowing at Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft and Oracle, and Goldman's SpaceX financing pitch is being framed as a way to help solve the broader AI debt puzzle facing underwriters.

The mechanism is that hyperscaler AI capex is increasingly funded with debt rather than pure free cash flow, and banks underwriting buyout and adjacent software financings are finding secondary demand thin enough that a position needs an 80-cent clearing price to move, a meaningful discount to par. That single-name discount is a sharper read on investor appetite for story-driven leveraged paper than the index-level OAS figures, which is why HY OAS at 3.52 and HYG down just 0.26% can sit calmly alongside a specific deal struggling to clear.

For leveraged finance desks, single-deal discounting like this is typically the leading indicator before stress shows up in broad spread indices. If more banks disclose similar marks on hung or difficult-to-place paper, underwriting standards for new buyout financing tighten just as AI capex borrowing from Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft and Oracle keeps investment-grade issuance supply elevated, creating a crowding dynamic between mega-cap tech paper and leveraged loan placement capacity.

Watch whether JPMorgan completes the $2 billion sale and at what final price, and whether other banks disclose comparable marks on AI-adjacent or buyout-related paper. A clearing price meaningfully below 80, or a cluster of similar disclosures, would confirm that financing stress is broadening beyond this one position rather than staying contained to a single hung deal.

Nikkei's 5% Drop Leads a Global AI Repricing

Japan's Nikkei fell 5.06% to 56,436.65, by far the sharpest move in today's global equity selloff, well ahead of the Eurostoxx's 2.86% decline and the Russell's 2.88% drop. OCBC frames this as the AI rout becoming a growth shock transmitted through EM currencies and a firm dollar. Gold fell 3.67% in the same session, an unusual pairing with falling equities that points to a deleveraging dynamic rather than a classic flight to safety.

Read more

The Nikkei's 5.06% drop to 56,436.65 dwarfed every other major index move in the session, against a 2.86% decline in the Eurostoxx 50, 1.58% in the FTSE, 0.95% in Shanghai, and 2.02% and 2.25% declines in the S&P and Nasdaq respectively. The VIX rose 15.8% to 28.44 and gold fell 3.67% to 322.87 in the same window, meaning the traditional safe-haven asset sold off alongside equities rather than against them.

OCBC's framing of the AI rout becoming a growth shock works through EM currencies, where a firm dollar at 122.65 on the DXY tightens dollar-funding costs for exporters and capital-equipment suppliers tied to the AI capex chain, a group Japan and Korea are heavily weighted toward, which helps explain why the Nikkei underperformed every other index by more than two full points. Gold falling alongside equities rather than rallying is the signature of broad deleveraging and a scramble for cash and dollars, not a rotation into traditional havens.

For credit, the transmission channel runs through dollar-denominated debt service at Asian tech supply chain issuers with capex-heavy balance sheets. If local currencies weaken further against a firm dollar, debt service costs rise for those borrowers even without any change in local policy rates, a tightening-financial-conditions channel distinct from but reinforcing the US story already visible in HY OAS up 1.44% to 3.52 and IG OAS up 1.08% to 0.94.

Watch Tokyo's next open for stabilization or continuation, and watch whether gold resumes acting as a hedge, since the WSJ has already flagged gold rebounding from its sharpest weekly decline since March. A continued divergence between falling gold and falling equities would be the more worrying combination, since it would suggest a cash and dollar liquidity squeeze rather than an AI-specific repricing.

WTI actually ticked up 0.32% to 89.53 even as a headline flagged AI bubble fears rattling crude. Integrated oil names are leading the group higher on the elevated price backdrop, while renewables are getting hit by the same price shock that is helping producers. The sector's relative calm looks less like conviction and more like a byproduct of oil holding near 90 while everything levered to growth and duration gets repriced.

Read more

The dominant story is dispersion: a market-wide selloff tied to AI bubble anxiety knocked 2%-plus off the major indices and sent the VIX up nearly 16%, but crude barely moved, with WTI up 0.32% to 89.53. One headline framed the session as oil sliding on AI fears, yet the settlement price tells a steadier story, consistent with another headline warning that global oil inventories are depleted enough that the next price spike could roil economies and markets. That inventory tightness is effectively a floor under crude even as equity risk appetite deteriorates elsewhere.

Integrated oil and gas names are doing the heavy lifting inside the sector, called out explicitly as riding a rally tied to the elevated price environment. The flip side of that trade is renewables, which one headline described bluntly as cursed by the same price shock benefiting producers, since higher crude and gas prices raise the relative cost of the energy transition trade rather than helping it. Shipping stocks are being floated separately as a way to play lower oil prices if they materialize, which tells you positioning is already split between those betting the current level holds and those betting it reverses.

The more relevant angle today is valuation and positioning rather than financing stress, since nothing in the tape points to energy-specific credit strain. One headline notes that oil stocks remain a good hedge but are no longer cheap, and another calls the sector's biggest money-making trade over while pointing investors toward alternatives. That combination, a hedge that has already re-rated plus explicit rotation talk, suggests the easy multiple expansion in integrateds is behind the group and future returns need actual commodity follow-through rather than just defensive positioning flows.

From here the setup hinges on whether crude's resilience survives a deeper equity drawdown. If the AI-driven risk-off broadens into real growth concerns, demand destruction fears could finally catch up to the price, undercutting the integrated-led outperformance. If instead the inventory depletion story headline is right and supply stays tight, any disruption could produce the opposite move, a price spike that reprices the entire sector higher regardless of what equities do. The next inventory print and whether broader indices stabilize are the two things to watch to see which scenario wins.
