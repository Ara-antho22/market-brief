Morning Edition

Wednesday, September 2, 2026

Newsletter Summary

Treasury yields are doing the driving this morning, with the 10-year at 4.75% (+0.42%) and the front end pinned at 4.34%, a combination that is dragging equities lower across the board: S&P 500 -0.71%, Nasdaq -1.03%, Russell -1.14%. Credit is following, not leading, with IG OAS up 1.27% to 80bps and HY OAS up 1.15% to 263bps, both moves small in absolute terms but directionally consistent with the risk-off tape. The VIX popped 3.4% to 14.92 and the dollar index rose to 118.75 as oil fell 2.83% to $83.90, an odd divergence given headlines tying crude to fresh Iran-strike inflation fears. Gold dropped 2.86% to $396.75, a classic real-yield casualty as nominal rates rise faster than inflation expectations. Underneath it all sits a Fed that held rates steady even as CPI printed at a three-year high, and an ADP report showing private payrolls up just 38,000 in August, the weakest pace since January. That combination, sticky inflation and softening labor, is exactly the stagflationary mix that makes rate-path pricing this volatile heading into Friday's jobs data.

Macro Update

Global bond rout pushes UST10 to 4.75%, spreads widen in sympathy

The 10-year yield rose 0.42% to 4.75% as part of a broader global bond selloff that CNBC and WSJ both tie to resurgent inflation fears, with oil-driven cost pressure cited as the proximate trigger. IG OAS widened 1.27% to 80bps and HY OAS widened 1.15% to 263bps, modest moves but the first sign that duration pain is bleeding into credit. HYG fell 0.89%, underperforming the broader equity decline on a beta-adjusted basis. This matters because higher long-end yields raise the discount rate on every future cash flow just as new issuance calendars for September open.

Read more

Treasury yields extended their climb overnight, with the 10-year at 4.75% and Japanese and other global sovereign yields also hitting multi-decade highs, according to Reuters and idnfinancials.com reporting. WSJ frames the move as oil prices fueling inflation worries, while CNBC and Yahoo Finance both describe an accelerating global bond rout rather than a one-day blip. Treasury Secretary Bessent nonetheless touted the bond market even as the 10-year yield spiked, a rhetorical stance that stands in some tension with the price action itself.

The transmission mechanism runs directly through the discount rate used on risk assets and through the relative cost of debt versus equity capital. When the risk-free rate at the long end rises this quickly, every leveraged capital structure faces a higher hurdle on refinancing, and equity multiples compress mechanically even absent any change in forward earnings estimates, which is a large part of why the S&P and Nasdaq are both down over 0.7% despite no earnings-specific catalyst today.

For leveraged finance specifically, the widening in HY OAS to 263bps and IG OAS to 80bps is the tell to watch. Both are still historically tight, but the direction of travel matters more than the level right now. If the bond selloff persists, new-issue concessions on high yield and leveraged loan deals will need to widen to clear syndicate, and floating-rate borrowers get partial relief only if SOFR keeps drifting down, as it did today at 3.66%, a small offset against a much larger move in long-dated benchmark yields.

The next confirming or disconfirming data point is Friday's nonfarm payrolls. A weak print alongside still-high yields would be the clearest sign that this is a term-premium and inflation-expectations story rather than a growth story, which is the more dangerous scenario for credit spreads since it removes the Fed's ability to cut into weakness without reigniting inflation fears.

Fed holds despite 3-year-high inflation, market prices a September hike

The Fed left rates unchanged even as inflation hit a three-year high, a decision ABC News frames as the Committee choosing to wait despite the price data. CNBC reports markets increasingly see incoming Fed Chair Warsh endorsing a rate hike in September, though not everyone on the desk is convinced, and PBS carries Trump saying Warsh will do what he has to do. That divergence between a hold today and a priced hike next month is unusual and it is showing up in the VIX, up 3.4% to 14.92, and in the dollar, up 0.33% to 118.75.

Read more

The Fed's decision to hold rates steady arrives alongside CPI data described as a three-year high, with the yoy print at 3.30% and the index itself barely moving month over month at 332.81, up just 0.07%. CNBC separately reports that markets are increasingly pricing a September hike under incoming Chair Warsh, a reversal of the cutting cycle the market had been positioned for, while PBS notes President Trump publicly framing Warsh's decision as something he will simply have to make regardless of political pressure.

The mechanism here is a policy-path repricing rather than a growth shock. Every basis point of upward revision to the expected terminal rate compresses the value of duration-sensitive assets immediately, which is why the two-year at 4.34% is holding flat while the ten-year moves, a curve behavior consistent with the market pricing near-term policy tightening without yet capitulating on the long-run inflation and term-premium story.

For credit desks, a hike-not-cut regime change matters more than the headline CPI number itself. SOFR at 3.66%, down slightly today, still anchors floating-rate leveraged loan coupons, and any hike would flow through directly to loan-market debt service coverage for the most levered issuers. It also complicates refinancing math for capital structures built on the assumption of a 2026 cutting cycle, forcing treasurers to reassess maturity walls against a higher-for-longer base case.

Watch Fed communication in the days ahead and any further Warsh commentary for confirmation. If the market's hike odds keep climbing into the September meeting, HY OAS and IG OAS, already up 1.15% and 1.27% today respectively, are the fastest tell that leveraged credit is repricing the same regime shift equities are still digesting through multiples rather than spreads.

ADP miss complicates the hike narrative just as jobs week begins

ADP reported private payrolls rose just 38,000 in August, the weakest pace since January and well short of expectations, according to CNBC, ADP's own release, and International Business Times. Unemployment separately fell to 4.10%, down 2.38%, an odd pairing that leaves the labor picture genuinely mixed heading into Friday's official jobs report. Barron's flags directly that this jobs data could complicate the Fed's rate hike plans, setting up a binary catalyst for the rates desk this week.

Read more

ADP's National Employment Report showed private-sector hiring of 38,000 jobs in August, a figure CNBC, ADP's own media center, and IBT all confirm as the slowest pace since January and below consensus expectations. That softness sits awkwardly against an unemployment rate that dropped to 4.10%, and against Staffing Industry Analysts' pre-release estimate that the economy likely added 55,000 jobs, roughly in line with the recent average but not by much.

The mechanism that matters here is the Fed's dual mandate collision. A hike case built on inflation running at 3.30% yoy requires labor to still be strong enough to tolerate tighter policy without a sharp growth hit. A 38,000 ADP print undercuts that premise, which is exactly why Barron's headline frames the jobs data as a complicating factor for hike plans rather than a confirming one, and why the front end at 4.34% is not moving even as the long end sells off.

For credit, this is the scenario that keeps issuers and underwriters nervous: a Fed forced to choose between validating inflation-driven hike pricing or backing off in response to a softening labor market, with spreads unable to fully price either outcome until the ambiguity resolves. HYG's 0.89% decline today reflects that uncertainty premium building into risk assets ahead of clarity, and any leveraged issuer with a September or October print window will be watching spread stability closely before locking terms.

Friday's nonfarm payrolls report is the explicit resolution point. A print materially below the ADP-implied pace would pull hike odds back down and likely stabilize the long end, while a strong headline number alongside sticky CPI would reinforce the Warsh-hike narrative and put further upward pressure on the 10-year, with knock-on widening risk for both IG and HY OAS.
