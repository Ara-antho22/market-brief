Macro Update

SoftBank's $11.1 Billion Print Meets a Widening Tape

SoftBank priced $11.1 billion of junk debt, a global record for corporate high-yield issuance, on the same day HY OAS widened 4.64% to 2.93 and IG OAS widened 2.53% to 0.81. Paramount Skydance is simultaneously marketing a $44.4 billion debt package for the Warner Bros. Discovery deal and Meta is preparing a European bond market debut, both tied to AI-linked fundraising. The mismatch between record primary volume and widening secondary spreads is the signal to watch this week.

Read more

SoftBank's $11.1 billion junk bond issuance sets a global record for corporate high-yield debt, arriving as HY OAS widened 4.64% to 2.93 and IG OAS widened 2.53% to 0.81 on the session, with HYG down 0.41% to 77.54. Paramount Skydance is out marketing a $44.4 billion debt package to fund the Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition, and Meta is set for a European bond market debut this fall as part of a broader shift in AI fundraising from equity to debt markets, a move TradingView frames as AI's $1 trillion funding problem migrating from stocks to bonds.

The mechanism here is straightforward: issuers are pulling forward volume and locking in size while the market can still absorb it, even as the price of that capital rises. Wider OAS means higher all-in coupons and lower proceeds relative to par for any fixed-rate tranche priced today, so a jumbo deal getting done does not mean it is getting done cheaply. For AI-linked issuers specifically, this is the borrowing bill referenced directly in credit commentary showing up as real spread widening rather than just headline risk.

The second-order effect is concentration risk building in the HY index around AI capex financing at precisely the moment spreads are moving wider, not tighter. That combination, record supply plus deteriorating secondary technicals, is a classic late-cycle setup where dealers can still place paper but at increasingly unfavorable terms for issuers, and where any risk-off shock hits recently priced AI paper disproportionately hard given its size and thematic clustering.

Watch whether Meta's European debut and the remainder of the Paramount Skydance package price inside or outside current talk levels, since that will confirm whether today's spread widening is a one-day move or the start of a repricing cycle. Also watch the trajectory of HY OAS through the rest of the week relative to the 2.93 print, since a continued climb would suggest the market is starting to discriminate against AI-capex paper specifically rather than pricing broad market risk.

10-Year Holds Above 5% as the Bond Market's 'New Era' Continues

UST10 sits at 5.17% and UST2 at 4.81%, both easing slightly today (-0.19% and -1.23%) after a multi-week climb that Reuters and CNBC describe as a march to multiyear highs and a new era for the bond market. VIX fell 4.44% to 14.21 even as S&P, Nasdaq, and Dow all declined, a divergence between equity options and rate-driven risk. SOFR remains anchored at 3.90%, meaning the pressure is concentrated in fixed-rate and long-duration paper rather than floating-rate credit.

Read more

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.17%, down 0.19% on the day, while the 2-year sits at 4.81%, down 1.23%, both pulling back modestly after what Reuters calls a battered bond market bracing for a new era of interest rates and CNBC describes as a march to multiyear highs. Equities fell in tandem, with the S&P down 0.77% to 7,683.69, the Nasdaq down 0.92%, and the Dow down 0.67%, while VIX dropped 4.44% to 14.21, an unusual pairing of falling stocks with falling implied equity volatility.

The transmission mechanism runs through the discount rate: a 10-year above 5% raises the required return on duration-heavy equity cash flows and directly sets the base rate for any fixed-coupon corporate bond priced today, which is why the Nasdaq underperformed the Dow on the session. SOFR holding flat at 3.90% means floating-rate leveraged loans are largely insulated from today's move, concentrating the repricing pressure on fixed-rate HY and IG issuance, exactly the kind of paper SoftBank, Meta, and Paramount Skydance are bringing to market this week.

A curve with 2s at 4.81% and 10s at 5.17% keeps term premium elevated, which is a headwind for duration-heavy IG portfolios and complicates refinancing math for capital structures carrying fixed-rate tranches priced off the long end. Citi's note that the Fed's October decision hinges specifically on September core CPI, rather than jobs or PCE data, means the market's reaction function has narrowed to a single print, which should keep realized rate volatility elevated even with VIX compressed at 14.21.

Watch September core CPI as the explicit swing factor Citi has identified for the Fed's October meeting, and watch whether the 10-year holds above 5% or breaks decisively in either direction following today's modest pullback. A clean break below 5% would ease pressure on new-issue pricing across the capital structure; a push higher would tighten financial conditions further into a week already carrying record HY supply.

Spain's CPI Jumps to 5%, Reinforcing a Global 'No Cuts Yet' Signal

Spanish inflation unexpectedly hit 5%, a three-and-a-half year high, and Australia's central bank left the door open to more hikes after already lifting rates to a 15-year high. Domestically, CPI YoY sits at 3.35% with the index up 0.40% month over month, and Citi says the Fed's October decision hinges specifically on September core CPI rather than jobs or PCE data. Belgium's inflation reached 4.69% in September on energy costs, underscoring that the sticky-inflation problem is not confined to one region.

Read more

Spanish inflation unexpectedly climbed to 5%, its highest level in three and a half years according to both WSJ and Bloomberg, backing the case for higher rates rather than cuts. Australia's central bank raised rates to a 15-year high and explicitly said more hikes are not off the table, per CNBC. Belgium's September inflation print came in at 4.69%, driven by energy prices, and Italian producer prices rose 3.1% in August. In the US, the CPI index sits at 334.13, up 0.40% month over month, with year-over-year CPI at 3.35% and unemployment steady at 4.10%.

The mechanism is that sticky inflation prints across Europe and Asia-Pacific reduce the odds of a coordinated global easing cycle, which keeps short-end rates and terminal rate expectations elevated even where growth data holds up. That is consistent with UST2 sitting at 4.81% and SOFR anchored at 3.90% with no near-term cut priced. Citi's framing that the Fed's October decision hinges specifically on September core CPI, not jobs or PCE, narrows the market's focus to a single data point and raises the stakes around that release.

For credit, sticky inflation plus a data-dependent Fed sustains a higher-for-longer discount rate, which shows up today in wider HY OAS at 2.93 (up 4.64%) and IG OAS at 0.81 (up 2.53%) even as issuers push record volume through the market via SoftBank, Meta, and Paramount Skydance. Energy-linked inflation in Belgium and Italy also keeps input costs elevated for leveraged borrowers in energy-intensive sectors, compounding refinancing risk for capital structures already facing wider spreads.

Watch the September US core CPI print, which Citi has identified as the explicit swing factor for the Fed's October meeting. Also watch whether Australia and other hawkish central banks follow through with actual additional hikes rather than just rhetoric, since that would confirm a genuine global tightening bias rather than a one-off Spanish print.

The dominant thread is geopolitical, not fundamental. The New York Times reports oil prices climbing on the continued impasse in U.S.-Iran talks, a supply-risk premium that is doing more to support crude than any demand signal from the macro tape, where the S&P fell 0.77%, the Nasdaq 0.92%, and the Nikkei 0.60% overnight. Audacy's wire on Asian equities falling while oil prices rise captures the cross-asset divergence directly: risk assets are being marked down globally even as crude finds a bid on the Iran headline risk. The WTI level in the data, 96.41 and down 0.58% on the session, suggests the overnight strength referenced in the Asian wrap had partially faded by the U.S. print, leaving a market that is jumpy on any signal out of the talks rather than trending cleanly in either direction.

Dispersion within the sector is best captured by the Yahoo Finance piece flagging oil stocks with direct Brent and WTI exposure as the names to watch, which implicitly separates pure-play upstream producers who move one-for-one with the crude tape from integrateds and midstream names with hedged or fee-based cash flows that are more insulated from the Iran headline swings. That distinction is why XLE's 0.10% gain looks calm on the surface while the underlying names likely showed a much wider dispersion, with direct-exposure E&Ps tracking the crude bid more tightly than diversified majors.

The financing story is the more durable one and it is constructive. The Motley Fool's reporting that oil companies are entering 2027 with their strongest balance sheets in years matters directly to leverage tolerance and M&A appetite in the sector: lower net debt and stronger free cash flow generation reduce refinancing risk at a moment when credit markets are re-pricing risk broadly, evidenced by HY OAS widening 4.64% to 2.93 and IG OAS widening 2.53% to 0.81 on the day. Energy issuers with clean balance sheets are positioned to be net beneficiaries of that widening, since they have less need to tap primary markets at the wider spread and more capacity to use the strength opportunistically for buybacks or consolidation, while weaker-balance-sheet E&Ps face a rising cost of capital just as HYG slipped 0.41% and the tone in high yield turned more cautious.

The setup from here hinges entirely on the Iran talks. Any escalation or breakdown would reinforce the supply-risk premium and likely push WTI back above the flat-to-down print seen today, benefiting the direct-exposure names Yahoo Finance highlighted, while a resolution or de-escalation would remove that premium quickly given crude is already showing signs of round-tripping intraday. Separately, watch whether the HY OAS widening seen today is a one-day move or the start of a trend, since that will determine whether energy's balance sheet strength becomes a source of relative outperformance or simply a buffer against a broader credit repricing.
