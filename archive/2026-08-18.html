Macro Update

30-Year Hits 5.31%, Worst Level Since 2007, on Iran and Fiscal Fear

The 30-year Treasury yield touched a 19-year high near 5.31% as Iran's threat to go fully offensive pushed WTI up 1.21% to $84.77 and reopened the global sovereign selloff already visible in UK and Japanese long bonds. China is trimming Treasury holdings into the same move. The mechanism is a term-premium repricing, not a policy shift, since the front end barely moved.

Read more

The 30-year yield reached its highest level since 2007 and the 10-year rose 1.08% to 4.68% in the same session that WTI climbed 1.21% to $84.77 on reports Iran is threatening a fully offensive posture, per Reuters' Morning Bid. Reuters separately reported eight Israeli airstrikes on Syria's Abu al-Duhur airbase with no casualties, and noted that the oil market is starting to price in a prolonged Hormuz disruption. The Financial Times and Reuters both frame this as a global bond selloff, not a US-specific one, with UK and Japanese yields also at multi-decade highs and China reportedly trimming its Treasury holdings amid the Iran situation.

This is a term-premium story rather than a rate-expectations story. SOFR sat unchanged at 3.62% and the 2-year rose only 0.48% to 4.17%, so almost the entire yield move is happening past the point where Fed policy expectations live. That means the repricing is coming from investors demanding more compensation to hold long-duration paper against fiscal issuance, sticky inflation, and now a live geopolitical shock to oil supply routes, which is exactly the combination that widens term premium without the Fed lifting the funds rate at all.

For financing desks, the split between rates and credit matters more than the headline yield. HY OAS actually tightened 1.48% to 2.67 and HYG was roughly flat at 79.61, while IG OAS widened 1.27% to 0.80, an inversion of the usual pattern where investment grade should be the more defensive asset. That divergence cannot hold if long yields keep climbing, since duration-heavy IG issuers absorb the term-premium move directly on new deals while HY borrowers with shorter tenors and floating-rate exposure are comparatively insulated. Watch for IG spreads to catch down toward the rate move, which would raise all-in coupons on new investment grade paper faster than on leveraged loan or HY issuance.

The read breaks if Iran de-escalates or if the Hormuz risk fails to materialize into actual shipping disruption, in which case the oil bid and part of the term-premium move unwinds quickly. The read is confirmed if China's Treasury holdings data shows continued reduction, if 30-year auction demand comes in soft, or if the Fed minutes this week acknowledge the long end as a financial stability concern rather than dismissing it as noise.

Oil, AI capex, and strong earnings are complicating the calculus, per equiti.com. The front end is telling a very different story than the back end, and that gap is the trade.

Read more

Coverage this week centers on a Fed rate decision with odds being tracked by defirate.com, a set of minutes due per US News & World Report, and WSJ identifying the 12 officials who vote on the decision. Chosun Ilbo's framing, that Treasury yields are surging despite a Fed rate freeze, captures the disconnect directly, and TradingView's read is that oil, AI investment, and resilient earnings have made the next move more complicated than a simple hold-or-cut call.

The mechanism is a curve split. SOFR held flat at 3.62% and the 2-year rose just 0.48% to 4.17%, meaning front-end pricing has not moved to reflect any near-term policy shift. The entire yield surge is concentrated at 10 years and beyond, up 1.08% and pushing the 30-year to a 19-year high, which is a term-premium and inflation-expectations story running independently of what the Fed does with the funds rate this week. CPI is running at 3.30% year over year, and the Guardian's framing of an inflation-rises-but-growth-slows dilemma is exactly the setup that keeps a central bank on hold while the long end reprices around it.

For financing desks this matters because SOFR-linked leveraged loan and TLB pricing is untouched by this move, while any fixed-rate, longer-duration HY or IG issuance is repricing against the 30-year's climb regardless of what the Fed says this week. The VIX fell 2.6% to 14.25, which suggests equity vol markets are treating this as a well-contained transition, but that complacency is a risk if the minutes reveal more hawkish dissent among the 12 voters than currently priced.

Watch the minutes release itself this week for language on the long end and financial stability, watch whether WTI holding above $85 shows up in the next CPI print, and watch whether any of the 12 voting officials break from consensus in public remarks. A hawkish surprise in the minutes would be the first sign the front end starts moving to catch up with the back end.

The Global Treasurer's coverage of a $489 billion AI debt wave lines up with bloomingbit's data showing global bond issuance down 16% from a year earlier at the aggregate level, while investment-grade corporate debt issuance is up 60% over the same period. Read together, the entire increase in corporate bond supply is being absorbed by a concentrated set of AI-infrastructure borrowers, not by the broader IG universe, which is otherwise seeing volumes contract.

The mechanism is that hyperscalers and AI infrastructure operators are terming out capital expenditure through bond markets rather than funding it entirely from operating cash flow, and they are doing so into a market where the discount rate just moved against them. IG OAS widened 1.27% to 0.80 in the same session that the 10-year rose 1.08% to 4.68% and the 30-year hit 5.31%, so new AI-linked paper is pricing at multi-year-high all-in coupons, which raises the return bar that AI capex has to clear to justify the debt load.

The capital structure implication is concentration risk sitting inside the IG index itself. Investing.com's 13F coverage notes SpaceX dominating endowment portfolios alongside signs of AI infrastructure equity selling emerging elsewhere, which suggests some investors are rotating away from AI equity exposure even as AI debt issuance accelerates. That combination, equity caution paired with debt-funded capex acceleration, is the classic setup where a monetization shortfall shows up first in credit spreads on the specific issuers rather than in the index average.

Watch whether IG spreads start to bifurcate, with AI-linked issuers trading wide of the broader IG universe rather than moving in line with it, and watch the pace and pricing of the next round of AI-linked bond deals for signs that investors are demanding a premium specifically for this cohort. A widening gap between AI-issuer spreads and the rest of IG would confirm the concentration read; a uniform IG move would suggest the market is treating this as a broad rate story rather than an issuer-specific one.
