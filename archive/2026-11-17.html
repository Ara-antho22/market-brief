Newsletter Summary

Equities sold off broadly, with the S&P 500 down 1.92% to 7,416.59, the Nasdaq off a sharper 2.49% to 24,509.76, and the Russell 2000 down 1.66% to 272.94. Credit moved the same direction but modestly: HY OAS widened 0.94% to 3.23 and HYG slipped 0.27%, while IG OAS held flat at 0.89, meaning the pressure so far is concentrated at the bottom of the capital structure. Gold fell 0.71% even as equities dropped, underscoring that this is a real-rate story first.

Macro Update

Nasdaq Underperformance Tracks the Move in Real Rates

The Nasdaq's 2.49% decline outpaced the S&P's 1.92% and the Dow's 1.41% as the 10-year yield held at 5.15% and SOFR rose to 3.93%. The single reason is duration: higher discount rates compress long-duration growth multiples disproportionately, and that mechanical effect is showing up hours before Nvidia reports. The VIX's rise to 19.30 reflects positioning into that print, not a broader fear gauge breakout.

Read more

Every major index fell Tuesday but the dispersion was telling. The S&P 500 dropped 1.92% to 7,416.59, the Dow 1.41% to 51,608.17, the Russell 2000 1.66% to 272.94, and the Nasdaq led all of them down 2.49% to 24,509.76. Overseas, the Nikkei fell 1.34%, the Euro Stoxx 1.67%, the FTSE 0.81%, and Shanghai 1.12%, confirming the selloff was not a single-market event. The dollar index ticked up 0.09% to 122.65 and gold fell 0.71% to 352.93, both consistent with a hawkish rates repricing rather than a flight-to-safety trade.

The transmission mechanism is straightforward duration math. When the 10-year yield sits at 5.15% and the 2-year at 4.88%, the discount rate applied to long-dated cash flows rises, and growth and tech names with earnings concentrated years out take the largest valuation hit. SOFR at 3.93%, up on the day, raises the carry cost on leveraged and margined equity positions simultaneously, which is why the Nasdaq's decline outran the broader market's rather than tracking it one for one.

The VIX's move to 19.30, a 3.21% increase, is consistent with hedging ahead of a known binary event (Nvidia's results after the close) layered onto a market already repricing the Fed path higher, rather than a standalone volatility event. In credit, the response was proportionate rather than acute: HY OAS widened 0.94% to 3.23 and HYG eased 0.27%, while IG OAS was unchanged at 0.89, showing the stress so far is confined to the lower-quality end of the market rather than spreading into investment-grade balance sheets.

The next read comes directly from Nvidia's print and guidance tonight, and from whether the 10-year holds near 5.15% or pushes through it on any hawkish Fed commentary this week. Confirmation of a deeper repricing would show up as HY OAS pushing past 3.30 and the VIX sustaining a move above 20 rather than fading back once the earnings event risk clears.

Sources: Reuters · South China Morning Post

Nvidia Turmoil Starts to Show Up in HY Spreads

Reuters framed Nvidia's volatility as marking the end of easy money for the AI trade, and HY OAS widened 0.94% to 3.23 alongside a 0.27% slip in HYG. The mechanism is that AI capex has been partly debt-funded, and as equity multiples on AI-adjacent names compress, the collateral cushion behind that financing narrows. IG OAS was unchanged at 0.89, so the repricing is still isolated to the lower-quality end of the credit stack.

Read more

Reuters' framing that Nvidia's turmoil marks the end of easy money for the AI trade landed the same day HY OAS widened to 3.23, up 0.94% on the session, and HYG eased 0.27%. Separately, Barclays flagged what it called a debt fairy tale creating repricing opportunities, and AMRO warned that the AI borrowing boom could push up global bond yields. IG OAS, by contrast, was flat at 0.89, indicating investment-grade issuers have not yet seen any spillover.

The mechanism connecting an equity-market AI wobble to credit spreads runs through financing structure. Much of the AI capex buildout, hyperscaler bonds, data center debt, and private credit facilities backing GPU and infrastructure spend, has been priced on the assumption that AI-linked revenue and equity valuations keep expanding. When that assumption cracks even modestly, as it did with Nvidia's volatility, investors holding the debt tied to that buildout start demanding more spread to compensate for the narrower equity cushion sitting beneath them, which is the exact concern AMRO raised about AI borrowing pushing global yields higher.

The second-order effect sits in the quality bifurcation already visible in the data: IG OAS unchanged at 0.89 versus HY OAS up to 3.23 suggests the market is treating this as a lower-tier credit story for now, not a broad investment-grade concern. That said, a Dividend.com piece arguing CCC bonds could be a bargain in high-yield credit suggests some investors are already positioning for a wider repricing across the ratings spectrum, which would show up first in primary market concessions for new HY and leveraged loan issuance.

The next confirming signal is whether HY OAS continues to widen past 3.30 in the sessions following Nvidia's results, and whether HYG's 0.27% slip extends into a sustained outflow trend. Watch new HY and leveraged loan primary pricing over the coming week for evidence that underwriters are having to concede wider spreads to clear AI-adjacent paper.

Sources: Reuters · Barclays Private Bank and Wealth Management · TNGlobal

Sticky Inflation Is Forcing the Hawkish Repricing

The CPI index rose 0.35% to 337.48, shoe prices hit record highs on tariffs per the New York Times, and Fed's Musalem said the US likely needs several more hikes by spring. Unemployment fell to 4.10%, giving the Fed cover to keep tightening rather than pivot. That combination, not an equity-market accident, is the root cause of today's move in rates, credit, and the dollar.

Read more

The CPI index printed 337.48, up 0.35% on the month, with PBS highlighting continued strain from food, energy, and housing costs even as Bloomberg reported gas pump prices at their lowest since March. The New York Times reported shoe prices at a record high tied to tariffs, illustrating that goods inflation is not fully offset by energy relief. Unemployment ticked down to 4.10%, a 2.38% decline, meaning the labor market gave the Fed no loosening to lean on. Fed's Musalem said the US likely needs several more rate hikes by spring, and the Chicago Fed separately warned that persistent high inflation would be painful to dislodge.

The mechanism is that with inflation still running above target and the labor market still tight, the Fed has no room to validate the rate-cut path that had been priced into risk assets. That shows up immediately in the rates complex: SOFR at 3.93% and the 2-year at 4.88% are both grinding higher as the market repriced the probability and magnitude of further hikes, and it is that move in the front end and in real yields that is doing the work in today's equity and credit selloff, not a standalone earnings or liquidity event.

The dollar index rose 0.09% to 122.65 ahead of the Fed decision, and a firmer dollar tightens financial conditions further for dollar-denominated borrowers, an effect visible in the mixed EM currency reaction described alongside the Treasury selloff. Within leveraged finance specifically, a higher-for-longer SOFR path raises the all-in cost of floating-rate leveraged loan paper at the same moment HY OAS is widening, a double cost increase for sub-investment-grade issuers trying to refinance into year-end.

The explicit next catalyst is the December Fed decision, which headlines are already framing as a certainty on direction with the only debate being magnitude. Watch for additional Fed speakers through the week reinforcing or softening the Musalem and Chicago Fed comments, and watch whether CPI components ex-tariff goods like footwear continue decoupling from the energy-driven disinflation Bloomberg flagged at the pump.

Sources: Morningstar · Reuters · The New York Times

The dominant story in health care today is relative stability, not any single headline. Dispersion within the sector is being set by these individual regulatory outcomes rather than a macro theme. From here the sector's relative performance is a function of how the broader equity selloff evolves rather than anything specific to health care. If the VIX keeps climbing, expect XLV to keep outperforming on a relative basis as it did today. The specific catalysts to watch are idiosyncratic: any appeal or refiling path for ivonescimab, follow-through in Roche shares on the breast cancer approval, and whether the Ollin acquisition signals J&J has more bolt-on capacity before year-end.
