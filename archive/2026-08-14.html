Macro Update

Soft CPI and Flat PPI Cement the Fed Hold, Spreads Grind Tighter

July CPI cooled to 3.30% year over year and PPI came in unchanged, reinforcing the consensus that the Fed leaves rates steady at its next meeting. Dallas Fed's Kaplan called the prior hold 'absolutely' the right call, and futures markets are pricing accordingly. HY OAS fell to 2.71 and IG OAS held at 0.79, both near cycle lows, while the VIX dropped 4.78% to 14.55, the market's clearest signal that a policy surprise is not expected in either direction.

Read more

July's CPI print showed inflation easing to 3.30% year over year, with CBS characterizing the report as a 3.4% annual pace, and the producer price index was flat on the month, both consistent with Reuters, WSJ, and CFO Dive coverage describing the data as dimming any residual case for a hike. Kaplan's on-record comment that the July hold was 'absolutely' correct adds a policy voice reinforcing the market read, and futures priced in Stocktwits coverage nudged higher into the decision on the same expectation.

The mechanism is straightforward: soft inflation data lowers the term premium embedded in longer maturities and anchors the front end, visible in the 2-year down 0.47% to 4.20% and the 10-year down 0.43% to 4.68% on the day, even with SOFR flat at 3.62%. A stable-to-lower rate path reduces the cost of carry for levered credit positions and widens the pool of buyers willing to reach for yield, which is the direct channel from a soft CPI print to spread compression rather than just a sentiment boost.

For financing conditions, HY OAS at 2.71 and IG OAS at 0.79 both sitting near multi-year tights means issuers can print at attractive all-in yields right now, and HYG's 0.23% gain points to continued inflows supporting that bid. This is the kind of backdrop that opens refinancing windows for stressed capital structures, relevant against the backdrop of the $150 billion software debt overhang flagged elsewhere in today's credit headlines, where issuers need exactly this kind of calm to term out maturities before any repricing.

The read breaks if wage growth reaccelerates without a matching productivity story, since marketplace.org's reporting on wages not catching up to slowed inflation is the loose thread here, real income pressure could still crimp consumption even as headline prints cool. Watch the next CPI and PPI releases and the Fed's actual decision date for confirmation that the hold thesis holds, and watch whether jobless claims, currently bouncing between 199k and 209k across differing reports, stay in a range consistent with a labor market that is cooling without breaking.

CNBC reports that the AI infrastructure financing boom is increasingly funded with leverage that is harder to track through private credit vehicles, arriving alongside a $150 billion software debt overhang flagged by Bloomberg's Paula Seligson and a live distress case in Braskem, whose ratings fell to C/D with debt talks underway despite $1.0 billion in EBITDA. Ellington Credit's CLO portfolio surged 8.5% amid active repositioning, and T. Rowe Price's OHA private credit fund kept distributions steady at $0.60 per share. Together these point to bifurcation building beneath a headline HY OAS of 2.71 that still looks calm.

Read more

CNBC's reporting describes AI infrastructure capex increasingly financed through leverage that sits outside public bond and loan markets, making it harder for investors to see buildup in real time. In the same window, Braskem posted $1.0 billion in EBITDA but saw ratings cut to C/D territory with debt restructuring talks underway, TradingView covered T. Rowe Price's OHA Select Private Credit Fund declaring $0.60 per share in distributions for the quarter, and Investing.com reported Ellington Credit's CLO portfolio up 8.5% amid active repositioning, while Seeking Alpha argued credit quality will be the differentiator in high yield through 2026.

The mechanism is that private credit and CLO structures absorb leverage tied to AI capex commitments off the public exchange, so stress in these positions does not show up immediately in the HY OAS print of 2.71 or in daily bond pricing the way a public high yield downgrade would. Ellington's 8.5% CLO gain reflects active repositioning inside that opacity, meaning managers are already reshuffling exposure ahead of any broader repricing, a signal that is only visible through fund-level disclosures rather than market spreads.

The second-order risk is that if AI-linked capex leverage sours, marks in private credit and CLO tranches could reprice with a lag and then move sharply once disclosed, unlike the continuous repricing public HY bonds undergo. Braskem's fall to C/D despite positive EBITDA is a live preview of how quickly a capital structure can unravel when debt service outpaces cash generation, a relevant comparison for software and AI-adjacent credits carrying the debt loads described in the $150 billion SaaS story.

What would confirm the risk is a downgrade cascade in AI-infrastructure or software credits currently held in private vehicles, or a discontinuous NAV markdown in a CLO or private credit fund similar to what showed up in Ellington's quarterly update. Watch subsequent private credit fund distribution reports for signs distributions are being maintained through asset sales rather than earned income, and watch how the Braskem debt talks resolve as a read on recovery rates for distressed leveraged credits more broadly.

Equiti's piece on whether markets are in an AI bubble sits squarely in this same debate, questioning whether current valuations are underwritten by durable cash flow or by capex commitments that assume continued access to cheap capital.

Dispersion within the sector tracks who controls unit economics versus who is exposed to input costs. That combination is exactly what allows a circular financing structure to keep rolling. The risk is that any widening in spreads or reassessment of AI-linked revenue quality would hit the funding side of this ecosystem before it hits the equity multiples.

The setup from here hinges on whether capex commitments start showing up as free cash flow drag without matching revenue proof, which is the crux of the bubble question raised by Equiti and echoed by Burry. A credit event is not imminent given where HY and IG spreads sit today, but the catalyst to watch is any hyperscaler earnings print or guidance cut that forces the market to separate durable AI infrastructure demand from capacity being built ahead of confirmed monetization. Until that happens, the tape will likely keep rewarding names perceived as cost-insulated, like Microsoft on energy, while punishing names where cost and trust questions are already surfacing, like Meta.
