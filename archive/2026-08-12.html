Macro Update

Hawkish Fed talk meets a market that already priced out hikes

Cleveland Fed's Hammack said multiple rate hikes may be needed to tame inflation and Chicago Fed's president called inflation a bigger threat than the labor market, even as coverage elsewhere describes September hike odds as having plunged. UST10 rose to 4.72% and UST2 to 4.25% overnight, with SOFR up to 3.64, all ahead of the print that will adjudicate which camp is right. The gap between Fed rhetoric and market pricing is itself the story, not the level of any single yield.

Read more

Two regional Fed officials pushed a more hawkish line overnight, with Hammack explicitly floating multiple additional hikes and the Chicago Fed president arguing inflation risk still dominates labor slack, even as separate coverage describes September hike odds as having plunged and frames the Fed's job as having become more difficult rather than easier. That divergence between what officials are saying and what futures markets are pricing sat behind the overnight move in rates, with UST10 up 1.51% to 4.72% and UST2 up 1.43% to 4.25%, both moving before the CPI data that is supposed to settle the argument has even printed.

The mechanism here is straightforward repricing risk. When Fed communication drifts more hawkish while market-implied hike odds are falling, the front end has to do the reconciling, and SOFR at 3.64 with a 0.28% move shows that reconciliation happening in real time in the funding market that leveraged loan coupons and CLO liability costs are indexed to. Every basis point added to that curve moves through to floating-rate credit faster than it moves through to term-out fixed coupon paper.

Spreads have not reacted at all, HY OAS flat at 2.70 and IG OAS flat at 0.78, which means the volatility so far is a duration story, not a credit story. That is the more dangerous setup for leveraged issuers precisely because it is quiet: if the hawkish camp is validated by today's data, the repricing hits the risk-free curve first and credit spreads second, and issuers with near-term maturities or floating-rate exposure feel the SOFR move well before they feel any OAS widening.

Watch the CPI print itself against consensus, and watch whether HY OAS starts to track the belly of the curve rather than sitting flat at 2.70. If Hammack's hawkish framing gets validated by a hot core number, the next place to look is SOFR futures and 2s10s for confirmation that the market is capitulating to the higher-for-longer camp rather than the Fed backing off.
