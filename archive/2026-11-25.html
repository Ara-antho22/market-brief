Macro Update

S&P Rips, Nasdaq Lags: The Rally Is a Rotation, Not a Rescue

The S&P's 4.73% advance masks a market that rotated hard out of the AI trade and into everything else, with the Nasdaq down 1.48% even as the Dow and Russell rallied. Nvidia-driven whiplash, flagged in CNBC's piece on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's attempt to read the signal through the noise, is still the swing factor for the index-level divergence. The VIX's 25.83% drop to 26.62 confirms a real de-grossing of hedges, but the level itself still implies above-average turbulence priced into options.

Read more

The S&P 500 gained 4.73% to 7,237.17 and the Dow added 0.85% to 50,018.70, while the Nasdaq fell 1.48% to 23,733.54. Reuters reported Nasdaq futures sliding more than 2% intraday as AI-related losses deepened, consistent with the index-level split: cyclicals and value names rallied while the concentrated AI winners that have driven index returns all year kept giving ground.

The mechanism is a rotation and a de-grossing happening simultaneously. Crowded long positioning in a handful of AI-linked names means that when that complex wobbles, funds sell what has worked to raise cash and buy or hold the rest of the market, which is why breadth measures like the Russell (+0.53%) and Dow can be positive on a day when the market's largest weighted names are falling. The VIX's sharp drop, down 25.83% to 26.62, shows that the hedges put on during the prior stress are being unwound faster than new ones are being added, which is itself a signal that positioning, not fundamentals, drove much of the prior selloff.

This matters for credit because spread compression tracked the equity rally rather than diverging from it: HY OAS tightened 3.46% to 3.63 and IG OAS tightened 1.02% to 0.97 on the same day. That is a relief rally transmitting across the capital structure, widening the issuance window for sponsors and corporates that had been waiting out the AI-driven volatility. HYG's flat print (-0.12%) suggests the cash bond ETF has not yet fully caught up to the spread move, which is typical when a rally is driven by derivatives and futures positioning before cash flows follow.

The read breaks if Nvidia-linked single-stock volatility reaccelerates or if the VIX stalls above current levels rather than continuing to normalize, which would indicate the de-grossing is incomplete. The read is confirmed if HY OAS keeps tightening through today's PCE print regardless of the inflation outcome, since that would show credit investors are pricing the rotation as durable rather than a one-day short squeeze.

Sources: CNBC · Reuters

The Fed Held Rates, But the Minutes Say Most Regional Boards Wanted a Hike

The Fed left rates unchanged even as inflation hit a three-year high, but minutes show a majority of regional Fed boards actually favored raising rates in October, and NY Fed President John Williams said higher rates are likely 'over time.' The CPI index rose 0.35% on the month to 337.48 while unemployment ticked down 2.38% to 4.10%, a combination that argues for hawkishness even as the headline decision looked dovish. Today's PCE print, the Fed's preferred gauge, and Q3 GDP data are the next checks on whether that hawkish undertone becomes the dominant narrative.

Read more

The Fed's hold was paired with internal dissent that only became visible through the minutes: most regional Fed boards wanted to raise rates in October, according to Bloomberg and corroborated reporting, even though the committee's public decision was to stand pat. Williams separately said on CNBC that higher interest rates are likely over time to address inflation. Both signals sit awkwardly next to a CPI index that rose 0.35% in the month to 337.48 and an unemployment rate that fell 2.38% to 4.10%, a backdrop that looks more like a case for tightening than for holding.

The transmission mechanism runs through front-end rate expectations first. SOFR rose 0.51% to 3.94, a modest but directionally hawkish move that reflects dealers pricing in some probability that the minutes and Williams' comments are a preview of actual policy rather than just rhetoric. If that repricing extends, it raises the cost of capital for anything priced off SOFR, which is the reference rate for the bulk of leveraged loan and CLO paper.

The second-order effect is on floating-rate credit specifically. A hawkish repricing at the short end raises debt service costs for issuers with unhedged floating-rate exposure well before it shows up in headline high yield spreads, which are more sensitive to default risk than to the level of base rates. Bloomberg's report on bond traders rushing to hedge the risk of Fed hikes stalling the economy points to the same tension: the market is simultaneously pricing cuts in places and hedging hikes in others, which is a classic sign of a committee that itself looks split, as the minutes confirm.

The read is confirmed if today's PCE print comes in hot and SOFR continues to climb, which would validate the regional boards' hawkish lean and likely pressure HY OAS wider from today's 3.63 print. It breaks if PCE comes in soft enough to let the Fed's public dovishness override the minutes, in which case Williams' comments get read as backward-looking rather than forward guidance.

Sources: ABC News · CNBC · Bloomberg · Bloomberg

Visma's €6.5 Billion Debt Sale Shows Sponsors Reading the Spread Window, Not the IPO Window

Hg is pursuing up to €6.5 billion of debt financing for Visma while putting the UK IPO option on ice, choosing the credit market over the equity market to fund or recap the asset. That decision lands on a day when HY OAS tightened 3.46% to 3.63 and IG OAS tightened 1.02% to 0.97, a compression that lowers the cost of locking in that financing now rather than waiting. It is a direct read on sponsor confidence in current high yield technicals even as the AI-driven equity volatility of the past week has been real.

Read more

Bloomberg reported that Hg is planning up to €6.5 billion in debt for Visma with the UK IPO shelved for now. That is a sizable single-name financing relative to European leveraged loan and high yield supply, and the choice to prioritize debt over an equity listing signals that the sponsor sees better execution and valuation certainty in credit markets than in a public offering right now.

The mechanism is straightforward: HY OAS at 3.63, down 3.46% on the day, and IG OAS at 0.97, down 1.02%, both compress the all-in coupon a borrower has to pay to clear the market. For a deal of this size, even a modest basis point improvement in spread translates into real savings over the life of the instrument, which is why issuers time large financings to days or weeks when spreads are grinding tighter rather than wider.

The second-order read is for the broader LBO and recap pipeline. If a transaction of Visma's scale can be debt-funded at tightened spreads while the equity market has been volatile enough to produce an 's&p up, Nasdaq down' session like today's, it suggests high yield and leveraged loan technicals, likely driven by scarcity of net new issuance and continued fund inflows, remain strong enough to absorb large single-name supply. That is constructive for the broader high yield primary calendar heading into year-end, when issuance windows typically narrow.

Confirmation comes from where the Visma debt ultimately prices relative to initial talk; pricing inside talk would confirm the technical strength implied by today's spread tightening. The read breaks if HY OAS reverses wider after today's PCE data, which would indicate the spread compression was a one-day beneficiary of the equity relief rally rather than a durable repricing of credit risk.

Sources: Bloomberg · The Dark Side Of The Boom

Hormuz Risk Builds, Crude Barely Moves

XLE added 0.97% to 60.45, outpacing a WTI gain of just 0.32% to 89.53 as headlines focused on Strait of Hormuz risk and political pressure on the White House to address rising crude prices. The equity move looks more like a beta trade off the broader tape, where the S&P 500 jumped 4.73% and the VIX dropped 25.83%, than a crude-driven repricing. Heating oil prices hitting new highs for Rhode Island consumers underscores that the pain from elevated crude is already showing up in downstream products even without a matching spike in the benchmark.

Read more

The sector narrative today is geopolitical rather than fundamental. Multiple outlets flagged rising Hormuz-related risk as the reason to screen for oil and gas names with price upside, while separate pieces asked whether the Trump administration can or will act to combat what headlines are calling a spike or surge in oil prices. That framing sits oddly against the actual tape, where WTI moved just 32 cents to 89.53, a gain that does not read as a spike by crude's own volatility standards. The disconnect suggests the risk premium is being priced in commentary and options skew ahead of any confirmed supply disruption, not yet in the spot barrel.

XLE's 0.97% gain outran oil itself, which points to energy equities riding the broader market rally more than reacting to a crude move. The S&P's 4.73% advance and the VIX's sharp 25.83% drop created a backdrop where cyclical and high-beta sectors like energy would be expected to participate regardless of the commodity. Separately, a screen of undervalued energy names after Q3 earnings put Par Pacific at the top of the list, and a Princeton divestment reversal piece noted energy stocks have returned 162% since the end of 2019, both reminders that valuation and long-only positioning narratives are circulating alongside the geopolitical headlines.

Credit conditions eased broadly today, with high yield OAS tightening 3.46% to 3.63 and investment grade OAS tightening 1.02% to 0.97. That move was not energy-specific in the headlines, but a sector with meaningful high yield issuance benefits disproportionately when spreads compress, since it lowers the cost of rolling reserve-based lending facilities and term debt. If the Hormuz risk premium holds crude above 85 without tipping into a genuine supply shock, that combination of firmer oil and tighter spreads is constructive for E&P and midstream balance sheets heading into any refinancing windows.

The catalyst to watch is whether Hormuz risk moves from rhetoric to an actual shipping or tanker incident that forces a real supply response, which would move crude well beyond today's 32 cent gain. Absent that, the setup is a market pricing in political risk and headline flow rather than a confirmed disruption, with equities tracking the broader rally more than the commodity. Any concrete policy action from Washington aimed at the price level, as several outlets are now asking about directly, would be the next thing that could decouple energy equities from the rest of the tape.

Credit spreads tightened on the same day, with HY OAS down to 3.63 and IG OAS to 0.97, underscoring that this is an equity positioning story rather than a funding stress one.

The session's dominant tech story was a reversal in sentiment on AI data center spending. That combination hit the semiconductor complex hardest within XLK, which fell 3.26% against a Nasdaq decline of just 1.48% and an S&P 500 that gained 4.73%, a gap that signals rotation out of chip and AI infrastructure names specifically rather than a tech-wide repricing.

Dispersion inside the sector is wide. Chipmakers tied to data center buildout bore the brunt of the selling, while hyperscaler fundamentals reported alongside the selloff look unambiguously strong: Alphabet disclosed $51.4 billion in capex alongside cloud revenue growth of 103%, and Microsoft's enterprise AI partnerships are drawing separate coverage on shareholder impact. Oracle is the standout decliner outside chips, down 4.5% on November 24, with 24/7 Wall St. framing it as a higher-leverage, cash-hungry twin to Microsoft, a label that puts its balance sheet discipline under scrutiny even as peers post stronger capex-to-revenue conversion.

The catalyst to watch is whether hyperscaler capex commentary, Alphabet's $51.4 billion figure and Microsoft's AI partnership economics among them, gets read as validating the spending cycle or as evidence of returns lagging outlay. Export policy on chip packaging is the other swing factor, given today's headline-driven focus on curbs. A stabilization in chip names without a reversal in credit spreads would confirm this as an equity-only repricing; a move wider in HY or IG alongside continued chip weakness would signal something more structural is starting to show up in the financing of AI infrastructure.

The question for the sector is whether today's beta underperformance persists if the broad risk-on move extends, or whether health care starts to catch up as a value laggard once the VIX stabilizes at these lower levels. On the regulatory side, the next catalyst to watch is early commercial uptake data for Ekitna and the other newly approved therapies, since launch trajectory rather than the approval headline itself is what will separate durable winners from one-day pops.
