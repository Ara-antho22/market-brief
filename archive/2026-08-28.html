Macro Update

Warsh Turns Hawkish, September Becomes a Coin Flip

Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks flagged inflation as still too high and said hikes may be needed, and CNBC now frames the September decision as a coin flip. The 2-year yield rose to 4.20% and the 10-year to 4.67% on the signal, per Wolf Street's reporting that 6-month to 3-year yields spiked. What matters most for financing desks is that this hawkish repricing has not dented risk appetite: the VIX fell to 14.51 and touched a fresh year-to-date low intraday.

Read more

Warsh told the Jackson Hole audience that inflation remains stubbornly elevated and that the Fed may have work to do, a message picked up across CNBC, PBS, AP, The Washington Post, The Guardian, and The New York Times. The market reaction was immediate and concentrated at the front end, with Wolf Street noting Treasury yields from 6 months out to 3 years spiked on the remarks, and the 2-year now sits at 4.20% versus the 10-year at 4.67%. CNBC's framing that September is now a coin flip reflects that rate-hike odds moved meaningfully off the prior baseline in a single session.

The transmission mechanism runs through the front end of the curve into the cost of new and refinanced leveraged debt. SOFR itself is flat today at 3.64%, since it is a backward-looking overnight rate, but the forward curve embedded in Fed funds futures is what actually reprices term loan spreads, revolver commitments, and any floating-rate paper being marketed this quarter. A hawkish repricing here raises all-in coupons on new issuance independent of what happens to credit spreads.

What is unusual is that HY OAS tightened 1.50% to 2.63 and IG OAS tightened 1.25% to 0.79 in the same session, and CNBC's own framing was that stock traders are warming up to Warsh rather than fearing him. That combination, higher hike odds paired with tighter spreads, reads as the market treating the hawkish pivot as confirmation of economic resilience rather than a policy mistake, a hike-into-strength trade rather than a hike-into-recession trade. For issuers this means primary market access likely stays open even as coupons rise, which is a different risk than a spread-widening freeze.

The next confirming data point is the September FOMC meeting itself and whether the coin-flip odds resolve toward a hike or hold. Watch for other Fed speakers to either reinforce or walk back Warsh's framing, and watch whether the resilient labor tone implied by initial claims falling to 203,000 this week continues, since a crack in labor data would flip the hike-into-strength narrative into a stagflation scare that credit spreads are not currently pricing.

Gold Craters, Oil Slides as Real Rates Reprice

Gold fell 3.24% to 408.89 and WTI dropped 2.83% to 83.90 in the same session that Warsh's hawkish remarks pushed Treasury yields higher, while the Minneapolis Fed confirmed that tariff pass-through to consumer prices has finally arrived. CPI is running 3.30% year over year, still above target. The move looks like a real-rate story rather than a dollar story, since DXY was essentially flat at 118.06.

Read more

Gold fell 3.24% to 408.89 and WTI slid 2.83% to 83.90 on the same day the 2-year yield rose to 4.20% and the 10-year to 4.67%. The Minneapolis Fed's research note stated plainly that the previously delayed pass-through of tariffs to consumer prices has arrived, and the CPI year-over-year print sits at 3.30% with the index itself up 0.07% on the month. Together these confirm the inflation backdrop Warsh cited in his speech.

The mechanism for gold is opportunity cost: a higher expected rate path raises the return on holding cash and short-duration Treasuries relative to a non-yielding asset, and gold's 3.24% drop is a direct repricing of that trade-off. The dollar index barely moved, down just 0.16% to 118.06, which rules out an FX-driven explanation and points to real yields as the proximate driver. Oil's decline likely reflects the growth-dampening implication of a tighter policy path rather than a supply story.

For leveraged issuers in energy and materials, this is a double squeeze: financing costs are rising on the front-end repricing while realized commodity prices are falling, which compresses the EBITDA cushions that covenant coverage tests and borrowing base redeterminations depend on. The Minneapolis Fed's confirmation of tariff pass-through also raises the bar for future CPI prints to surprise to the downside, which keeps the hawkish option alive for the Fed and keeps this repricing pressure in place rather than reversing it.

Watch the next CPI print to see whether tariff pass-through broadens beyond the categories the Minneapolis Fed flagged, and watch whether gold's move stabilizes or extends, since a continued slide would confirm the market believes the rate-hike repricing is durable rather than a one-day reaction to a single speech.

AI Debt Issuance Meets a Rising-Rate Backdrop

PIMCO warned that hundreds of billions in AI-linked tech bond issuance is causing indigestion and pushing Treasury yields higher on its own, a supply-side pressure sitting on top of the Warsh-driven repricing. Zero-interest convertible bond issuance is on pace for a record year per the FT, even as HY OAS tightened to 2.63 and IG OAS tightened to 0.79. That combination is a bifurcated market pricing rate risk and credit risk very differently right now.

Read more

PIMCO's warning, reported by finance.biggo.com, is that tech giants' issuance tied to AI capex financing is large enough to push up Treasury yields through pure supply pressure, independent of Fed policy. The FT separately reports zero-interest convertible bonds are on pace for a record year, a sign that issuers with richly valued, volatile equity can still print cheap paper by embedding optionality. Both are happening while HY OAS sits at 2.63, tighter by 1.50%, and IG OAS sits at 0.79, tighter by 1.25%.

The mechanism is that heavy investment-grade tech supply competes directly with Treasuries and other IG paper for duration-focused buyers, lifting term premium at the long end through a supply-demand channel that is distinct from the front-end move tied to Warsh's rate-hike signal. Zero-coupon converts sidestep that pressure entirely by monetizing equity volatility instead of paying a cash coupon, which is why that market can hit a record even as base rates rise elsewhere.

For leveraged finance specifically, this is the tape to watch: spread compensation is not widening, meaning the market is not demanding more credit protection, but the risk-free rate underneath that spread is moving higher on two separate forces, Fed policy and Treasury market supply-demand mechanics tied to AI financing. The practical effect is that all-in yields on new leveraged issuance are climbing even though the market's assessment of default risk, as expressed in OAS, has not changed.

The thing to watch is whether investment-grade tech supply calendars stay heavy into September and whether that keeps pressuring the long end independent of the Fed, and whether HY OAS begins to widen if the hike-into-strength narrative from Warsh's speech starts to crack under actual data.

Sources: finance.biggo.com · Financial Times

Any headline that calls the Hormuz status back into question would reverse today's move quickly given how much of the recent price action has been premium-driven rather than fundamentals-driven. Absent that, the setup favors names with capital discipline at current strip prices over pure beta plays, since the market is already showing it will differentiate on the way down.

A newly approved cardiovascular indication for Mounjaro strengthens Lilly's cash flow visibility, which matters for its capacity to keep funding its own pipeline and any bolt-on M&A without stressing its balance sheet. For smaller approval-stage names, tighter spreads and lower VIX make it a better window to term out debt or price a follow-on than it has been in weeks, and platform companies like Roivant that depend on capital markets access to fund multiple pipeline bets benefit disproportionately from any sustained compression in risk premia.

The setup from here hinges on whether this week's approval density translates into commercial data that the market can underwrite, since approvals without uptake evidence tend to fade as catalysts within a quarter. Watch Mounjaro prescription trends and payer coverage decisions following the cardiovascular label expansion as the next real test of whether the approval repriced Lilly's growth trajectory or was already priced in. On the financing side, continued compression in HY and IG OAS would keep the funding window open for smaller biotech issuers to lock in lower coupons, while any reversal in that spread tightening would hit the small-cap approval names hardest given their thinner cash runways.
