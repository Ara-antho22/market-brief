Macro Update

Fed Minutes Show Hawks Gaining Ground Just as Powell Extends His Stay

FOMC minutes revealed broader internal support for additional rate increases if inflation fails to cool, a notably more hawkish signal than markets had been pricing. The release lands alongside news that Powell will remain on the Fed's board after his chair term expires next month, and against a backdrop of Trump publicly pushing for lower rates. The mismatch between political pressure and committee tone is the story to track into the next meeting.

Read more

Multiple outlets including CNBC, WSJ, Axios and The Hill converged on the same minutes takeaway: a broader bloc of officials now sees a case for hiking further if inflation doesn't cool, a shift from the cut-leaning tone markets had priced. That lands the same week Powell confirmed he will stay on the Fed's board after his chair term expires next month, preserving institutional continuity even as Trump continues to publicly call for lower rates, and as officials separately tread carefully in the wake of Treasury's bond market intervention.

The mechanism runs through the front end first. Hawkish minutes recalibrate the expected path of the policy rate, which is why the 2-year sat unchanged at 4.19% while the 10-year actually fell 1.27% to 4.65%, a flattening bias that reflects the market betting the long-run disinflation story still holds even if the near-term path gets bumpier. Equities absorbed the repricing directly, with the S&P down 0.87%, the Dow off 1.32%, and the Russell underperforming at -1.34%, since a higher-for-longer discount rate compresses multiples fastest in smaller, more rate-sensitive names.

Credit did not confirm the equity move. HY OAS tightened 0.73% to 2.73 and IG OAS tightened 1.22% to 0.81, with SOFR easing slightly to 3.62%, meaning the financing tape for leveraged issuers stayed constructive even as the equity narrative turned hawkish. That divergence matters for anyone financing a deal this week: primary HY and leveraged loan pricing takes its cue from spread levels more than from the day's equity tape, so a hawkish minutes headline that doesn't move OAS wider is not yet a reason to widen new-issue concessions, though it does raise the floating-rate cost of carry for sponsor-backed borrowers if the committee follows through.

The read gets confirmed or broken by the next CPI print against the 3.30% year-over-year base and by whether Fed speakers turn the minutes' hawkish language into explicit forward guidance. Watch fed funds futures for repricing of hike odds and watch whether HY and IG OAS start drifting wider in sympathy with equities, since that would be the signal that credit is finally catching up to the hawkish surprise rather than shrugging it off.

Treasury Doubles Down on Buybacks as Yields Refuse to Cooperate

Bessent said the current Treasury buyback operation could exceed $4 billion and could be upsized further, doubling down on a program aimed at smoothing bond market functioning. The move follows an earlier Treasury intervention that Fed officials are now navigating carefully. Yields are proving stubborn regardless, which is the point worth tracking for anyone pricing off the belly of the curve.

Read more

Bessent confirmed the buyback operation could run north of $4 billion and signaled room to increase it further, part of a broader pattern of upsized Treasury buybacks even as yields have rebounded rather than fallen. Reuters separately reported that Fed officials are treading carefully in response to Treasury's earlier bond market intervention, suggesting some institutional tension over how aggressively Treasury should be managing the curve versus letting the Fed's own policy stance do the work.

The mechanism here is liquidity management, not monetary easing. Buybacks retire off-the-run, less liquid issues and are designed to smooth market functioning and reduce volatility in auctions, but they do not materially change the net supply-demand balance driving the level of yields. That is why the 10-year sits at 4.65%, down only modestly on the day, even as the buyback program itself has been expanding, a sign the operation is targeting market plumbing rather than the rate level.

For financing markets, the read-through is about volatility rather than direction. If buybacks succeed in dampening intraday and auction-cycle swings in Treasuries, that stability supports tighter and steadier pricing in HY and IG primary, consistent with today's compressed OAS levels. But Bessent's parallel framing that the US can grow its way out of $40 trillion in debt implies a persistent, heavy issuance calendar that will keep competing with corporate borrowers for duration demand, a dynamic flagged separately by TD Economics on rising corporate borrowing meeting a broader investment cycle.

Watch the size and frequency of the next several buyback operations and whether the 10-year stabilizes below 4.65% or resumes climbing despite the intervention. Also watch for any explicit Fed commentary on the Treasury operations, since the Reuters piece on officials treading carefully suggests the central bank is watching this as closely as the market is.

China is instead boosting Russian crude imports, complicating enforcement and keeping supply risk in the price. The timing is inconvenient for a Fed already debating whether hikes are needed if inflation doesn't cool.

Read more

Oil is set for a second weekly rise as unsettled US-Iran tensions crimp supply expectations, with the US confirming plans to impose the toughest sanctions in history on Iran and Bessent explicitly urging China to cooperate with enforcement. Reuters separately reported China is instead boosting imports of Russian crude, a move that complicates the sanctions regime and stymies India's refiners, meaning the physical oil market may not tighten as cleanly as the sanctions headline implies.

The transmission channel to markets is direct and fast: sanctions threats reduce expected prompt physical supply from a major producer, which lifts front-month crude even before enforcement is confirmed. That energy channel is already visible in Japan, where headline inflation hit its highest level this year on energy prices, a preview of how sticky energy costs feed directly into headline CPI prints elsewhere.

For the Fed, rising energy costs arrive at the worst possible moment, layering on top of a minutes release that already showed broader committee support for hikes if inflation doesn't cool. For credit, the effect bifurcates: energy-linked HY issuers get a cash flow tailwind from higher crude, a supportive dynamic for that corner of the high yield index, while consumer-facing and lower-margin issuers face renewed input cost pressure, consistent with Walmart's separately reported weakest growth in years as inflation-fatigued consumers pull back.

The next confirmation point is whether the sanctions are actually implemented and how China responds to Bessent's cooperation request, since continued Russian crude flows into China would blunt the intended supply effect. Watch whether WTI holds above the 86 level into next week and how that feeds through to the next CPI print against the current 3.30% year-over-year base.

Quality sponsors with occupied, well-located assets like Tysons can refinance or sell at reasonable marks. Sponsors holding dated, partially vacant stock are still facing lender-driven dispositions.

The setup from here is a bar-belled office market where continued rate relief keeps compressing cap rates and easing refi stress for trophy and medical-anchored assets, while distressed sales of older stock keep clearing the market at depressed values and effectively repricing the sector's floor. The catalyst to watch is the pace of further UST and SOFR declines, since incremental easing disproportionately helps floating-rate and maturing office borrowers, alongside any follow-through in the NAR and CoStar secondary-market demand indices that would confirm the migration story is durable rather than a short-term artifact.
