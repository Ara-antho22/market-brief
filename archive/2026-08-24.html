Macro Update

Warsh's Jackson Hole Debut Is the Week's Real Catalyst

New Fed chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole speech this week with UST10 at 4.69% and the VIX up 7.52% to 16.01, both signaling markets are bracing for guidance risk rather than resolution. Bloomberg and Reuters both frame the setup as one where the long end sells off further absent clear direction. Warsh is characterized elsewhere as a supply-side pessimist, a framework the market has not yet had to price against an actual policy path.

Read more

Bloomberg's framing is explicit: US long bonds risk a deeper selloff without clear Warsh guidance, and Reuters separately notes bond market anxiety has raised the stakes for his debut Jackson Hole appearance. UST10 is at 4.69% (+0.86% on the day) and the VIX jumped 7.52% to 16.01, both consistent with a market that is under-hedged for a hawkish surprise rather than complacent about a dovish one. The Newsweek piece framing this as five questions Warsh must answer underscores that his reaction function is still unknown to the market.

The mechanism runs through duration and term premium rather than the policy rate itself. A new chair with an unclear framework increases uncertainty about the entire forward curve, not just the next meeting, and that uncertainty is priced disproportionately into the long end because duration amplifies any repricing of the terminal rate or the inflation risk premium. Equities can shrug this off in the near term because near-dated cuts are still broadly priced, but the long end has no such anchor, which is exactly why UST10 is doing more of the work than the 2-year, flat on the day at 4.19%.

The credit market is already leaning risk-off into the event. IG OAS widened 1.23% and HY OAS widened 0.73% in the same session, which raises the all-in cost of new issuance independent of any change in underlying credit quality. For leveraged issuers with refinancing on the calendar, a hawkish surprise from Warsh would compound an already rising base rate with wider spread, a double hit to coupon that shows up first in interest coverage ratios for the most levered capital structures.

Watch the content of the speech itself: any explicit reference to the long end, to balance sheet policy, or to a framework for tolerating above-target inflation would be the confirming signal. The break case is a speech that avoids specifics entirely, which based on the setup described by Bloomberg and Reuters would likely extend the long-bond selloff rather than resolve it.

Gold's Breakout Is a Hedge Trade Into PCE, Not a Verdict

Gold jumped 1.95% to $423.36, a more than three-month high, ahead of US inflation data and the Fed chair's speech, per Reuters. CPI yoy is running at 3.30% with the index itself barely moving month over month (+0.07%), which keeps the inflation debate unresolved heading into this week's PCE release flagged by CoinDesk's calendar. The move looks like positioning into event risk rather than confirmation of a reflation trend.

Read more

Reuters reports gold hit an over three-month high ahead of US inflation data and the Fed chair's speech, consistent with today's 1.95% move to $423.36. That sits against a backdrop where CPI yoy is at 3.30% and the CPI index rose just 0.07% on the month, a combination that leaves the year-over-year print sticky without fresh monthly acceleration. CoinDesk's week-ahead piece places the Jackson Hole symposium and US PCE prices on the same calendar, meaning gold buyers are front-running two catalysts at once.

The mechanism is a real-rate and dollar story more than a pure inflation-expectations story. DXY is down 0.24% at 118.90, and a softer dollar combined with sticky above-target CPI compresses the real yield gold competes against, making the metal more attractive as a store of value precisely when nominal yields like UST10 at 4.69% are already elevated. That combination, a weak dollar alongside high nominal but uncertain real yields, is the specific setup where gold outperforms both bonds and cash.

For financing markets, the persistence of 3.30% yoy inflation matters because it caps how aggressively the Fed can ease even if growth data softens, which keeps SOFR anchored near 3.63% and floating-rate obligations expensive for leveraged borrowers. Any leveraged loan or CLO exposure priced off SOFR does not get relief from a Fed that is data-constrained on the inflation side, which is the direct read-through from a gold market that is hedging rather than declaring victory on disinflation.

The PCE print this week is the actual test. A print that confirms deceleration back toward target would likely see gold give back today's gain as the hedge unwinds; a hot print would validate the move and likely extend the widening already visible in HY OAS and IG OAS. Watch whether gold holds its gain into and through the data, not just on the day before.

The AI Debt Boom Is Repricing Everyone's Cost of Capital

24/7 Wall St. reports the AI-driven debt boom is pushing borrowing costs higher across the market, and SoftBank is planning a $6.3 billion retail bond to fund Masayoshi Son's AI bet, tapping Japanese retail investors per TradingKey. This is landing on a day when both IG OAS (+1.23%) and HY OAS (+0.73%) widened, alongside UST10 at 4.69%, meaning new supply is arriving into a market where the base rate and the spread are both under pressure simultaneously.

Read more

24/7 Wall St. frames the AI debt boom as a broad-based force lifting the cost of capital, not just for AI-adjacent issuers. TradingKey reports SoftBank plans a $6.3 billion retail bond issue specifically to fund Son's AI investments, a structure that relies on Japanese retail demand rather than traditional institutional order books. This is happening against UST10 at 4.69%, which the Intellectia AI pieces separately describe as a 19-year high alongside a national debt figure surpassing prior records.

The mechanism is crowding out through sheer issuance volume. When hyperscalers and AI-adjacent borrowers add heavy new supply to a market that is simultaneously digesting elevated Treasury issuance tied to the fiscal deficit, the clearing yield on new corporate paper rises even absent any change in the issuer's credit quality, because the base rate the deal prices off is climbing and there is more paper competing for the same pool of duration-tolerant capital.

Both IG OAS and HY OAS widened in the same session, 1.23% and 0.73% respectively, which is the direct evidence that this is showing up as spread, not just yield. For leveraged issuers this means higher coupons on any refinancing executed into this window, and it compresses interest coverage precisely for the capital-intensive infrastructure buildouts, including AI-related capex, that are most reliant on continuous access to the debt market to fund construction ahead of revenue.

Watch the SoftBank retail bond pricing relative to guidance as a live demand signal, and track whether IG OAS and HY OAS continue to widen through the week or stabilize once Warsh's Jackson Hole speech removes some policy uncertainty. A continued widening alongside heavy AI-linked issuance would confirm that the cost-of-capital story is structural rather than a one-day risk-off move.

The setup from here hinges on whether the next round of hyperscaler earnings and capex guidance validates the sovereign AI demand offset or confirms the cash flow limit thesis. AMAT, Lam, and KLA remain the cleanest read on whether equipment order books keep pace with the spending figures being debated, while rare earth names are now trading as a tell on sentiment fragility rather than fundamentals. The catalyst that would reprice this fastest is a hyperscaler either raising 2026 capex guidance further, which would pressure credit spreads on tech issuers already drifting wider, or pulling it back, which would validate the cash flow limit argument and hit the equipment cohort that has run hardest.

Watch the 10-year: a sustained move above 4.70% percent tightens the arbitrage for both REIT acquisitions and CMBS conduit execution, while any relief in the delinquency data next quarter would be the clearest sign the office capitulation is bottoming rather than just beginning.
