Macro Update

The Fed Hiked and Risk Rallied Anyway

The Fed's first hike in three years is being read by equities as a credibility signal rather than a growth threat, with Boston Fed's Collins backing the move and flagging another hike in 2026 while Goolsbee warns the disinflation path won't be painless. Credit spreads tightened on the news rather than widening, even as the front end repriced hard. The disconnect is the two-speed economy Kevin Warsh described, AI capex financed cheaply while housing-sensitive borrowers take the hike in full.

Read more

The Fed delivered its first interest rate increase in three years, and the response across the Fed itself was united hawkishness rather than dissent. Boston Fed President Susan Collins said she backed the hike and expects another one in 2026, while Chicago Fed's Goolsbee cautioned that the road back to 2% inflation may not be painless. Kevin Warsh's commentary framed the backdrop as a two-speed economy with AI and housing sitting at opposite poles. The market move confirms the front end took it seriously: two-year yields rose 1.93% to 4.76% and the ten-year climbed 1.42% to 5.01%, while equities still posted a 1.49% S&P gain and a 2.26% Nasdaq gain.

The mechanism here is that a hike delivered with explicit forward guidance, another one flagged for 2026, gets treated by risk assets as the Fed reasserting control over inflation expectations rather than as a demand-destruction signal. That is consistent with the WSJ framing of the Fed regaining trust. Credit spreads tightening alongside a hike, HY OAS down 0.74% to 2.68 and IG OAS down 1.28% to 0.77, tells you the market is pricing higher nominal rates without pricing a recession, which is a benign combination for spread product even as all-in yields for issuers keep climbing.

The second-order effect is that borrowing math is now genuinely different for corporates sitting on the maturity wall. A company refinancing into this curve pays SOFR at 3.85% plus a spread that is historically tight, but the base rate itself is higher than it has been in years, so total coupon cost rises even as credit risk premium falls. That divergence lets AI-adjacent capex keep getting financed on favorable terms while housing-linked and consumer-cyclical issuers face the full transmission of the hike into their refinancing economics.

Watch whether Collins's projected 2026 hike gets corroborated by other Fed voices, and whether HY OAS reverses wider once housing-sensitive credits start reporting the hike's effect on interest coverage. The new issue calendar this week is the tell, if concessions stay thin on IG and HY deals despite the higher base rate, the market is still buying the credibility story.

Sources: WSJ · qz.com · fortune.com · observer.com

Crude's Iran Premium Reopens the Inflation Trade

WTI jumped 4.49% to $107.02 on Iran-related headline risk just as Forbes warns Iran could take the global economy hostage on energy prices. WSJ is flagging the risk of oil delivering a second inflation strike right as CPI sits at 3.35% year over year and the Fed has just signaled another hike is coming. Trump is set to defend the Iran war at the UN even as Reuters reports both sides hinting at a revival of talks, so the headline risk cuts both ways.

Read more

WTI rose 4.49% to $107.02 as Iran-linked geopolitical risk intensified, with Forbes warning Iran could take the global economy hostage through higher energy prices and inflation. Trump is set to use his UN speech to defend the Iran war while touting peace efforts, and Reuters separately reports Iran and the US hinting at a revival of talks as Trump and Pezeshkian both attend the UN. CPI is running at 3.35% year over year with the index at 334.13, up 0.40% month over month, meaning any sustained oil move feeds directly into a headline print the Fed is already watching closely.

The transmission mechanism is direct: energy is a headline CPI component, and a sustained WTI move above $100 raises breakeven inflation expectations at the exact moment the Fed just justified a hike partly on inflation persistence grounds, per Collins and Goolsbee. That creates a feedback loop risk, higher oil pushes up realized and expected inflation, which reinforces the case for the additional 2026 hike Collins already flagged, which raises the discount rate applied to every duration-sensitive asset even as today's equity tape rallied on unrelated AI headlines.

For credit, the near-term effect is bifurcated. Energy-linked issuers get a cash flow tailwind that should support their spreads within an HY index already tight at 2.68. But the broader corporate universe refinancing into a curve where the ten-year sits at 5.01% and the dollar is firm at 119.51 faces a scenario where an oil-driven CPI surprise forces the Fed to lean more hawkish than currently priced, raising all-in financing costs for the entire maturity wall regardless of individual credit quality.

The next CPI print is the direct test of pass-through from this WTI spike. Also watch the UN this week for whether Trump's Iran war defense and the Reuters-reported talks revival move in the same direction or contradict each other, since the geopolitical premium in oil can unwind as fast as it built. If HY OAS starts widening off the current 2.68 print, that is the signal the energy shock is bleeding into broader credit risk rather than staying contained to energy names.

Sources: WSJ · Forbes · Reuters · Reuters

AI Credit Cracks Beneath the Equity Rally

Nasdaq hit an intraday record on Alphabet strength, but the credit backing AI capex is telling a different story: Oracle's Project Jupiter loans have fallen below 90 cents, SoftBank had to pay a yield near 10% on the longest tranche of its oversubscribed $10 billion-plus junk bond, and bond investors are demanding higher yields from Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia. The equity and credit markets are pricing the same AI capex boom in opposite directions.

Read more

The Nasdaq rose 2.26% to a fresh intraday record on an Alphabet boost, per Reuters, the same day TradingView reported that bond investors are demanding higher yields from Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia as big tech's AI debt spree hits a wall. Separately, Oracle's Project Jupiter loans have fallen below 90 cents on the dollar as AI credit meets the real world, and SoftBank priced an oversubscribed junk bond north of $10 billion with the longest 7.5-year tranche testing a yield near 10%, a deal Yellow.com frames as deepening SoftBank's OpenAI-linked debt risk.

Leveraged loan and junk bond prices are forward-pricing mechanisms for cash flow risk in a way equity multiples are not. A floating-rate loan trading through 90 cents despite carrying no duration risk implies the market is discounting either slower AI monetization or refinancing risk on the underlying project, a signal equity investors buying the same capex story on growth optionality are not yet reflecting. A near-10% yield on a 7.5-year tranche, even with the deal oversubscribed, tells you demand exists only at a coupon that prices real credit risk, not a benign spread.

This is dispersion hiding inside tight aggregate numbers. HY OAS at 2.68 and IG OAS at 0.77 look constructive in the index, but that tightness is being carried by non-AI credits while AI-adjacent paper reprices wider underneath, a classic late-cycle signal. For leveraged finance desks this sets the marginal clearing price for the next AI-linked term loan B or unsecured bond, and it will shape how underwriters size and price the broader corporate maturity wall refinancing into a market where hyperscaler-adjacent credit is now visibly diverging from IG tech equity performance.

Watch secondary levels on Oracle's Project Jupiter loans, a move further below 90 would confirm the stress is spreading rather than idiosyncratic. Watch whether SoftBank's oversubscription holds through final allocation and pricing, and watch for read-through into any forthcoming Alphabet, Meta or Nvidia primary bond deals, since wider concessions there would confirm the credit market is now pricing AI capex risk independently of the equity rally.

Yet WTI finished the day up 4.49% at 107.02, meaning whatever intraday relief rally in risk sentiment showed up in stocks did not hold in the oil pit, or the diplomacy-driven selloff reversed sharply into the close. The EIA's reminder that public companies produce the majority of US crude and gas underscores that this financing dynamic touches the bulk of domestic supply, not a fringe of independents.

The immediate catalyst is the US-Iran diplomatic track itself. If talks produce a credible de-escalation, the MarketWatch framing suggests energy equities and oil prices both take a leg down, testing whether the insider buying reported there was early or premature. If talks stall or the geopolitical premium reasserts, the whipsaw seen today between falling Brent headlines and a rising WTI settlement could repeat, and XLE's flat print becomes less representative of the volatility underneath it. Watch whether HY and IG spreads continue tightening in tandem with oil, since a divergence there would be the first sign that credit is pricing a different outcome than equities.
