Friday, November 13, 2026

Newsletter Summary

The S&P 500 climbed 1.31% to 7,619.38 while the Dow slipped 0.36%, the Nasdaq slipped 0.39%, and the Russell 2000 dropped 1.64% to 278.44, a split that says a handful of large caps did the index's work today. The move came against a genuine rates story: the 10-year yield rose 2.05% to 4.98% and the 2-year rose 3.73% to 4.73% after Fed officials Waller and Hammack publicly disagreed on whether the next move is a 50bp hike or a gradual approach, with CNBC framing it as the Fed pivoting from expected cuts back into an inflation fight. Credit did not flinch. HY OAS tightened 1.20% to 3.30 and IG OAS tightened 1.10% to 0.90, both near their tights, even as the risk-free rate climbed under them. VIX fell 10.12% to 17.49, consistent with an equity options market that is not pricing the divergence underneath the headline index as a risk event yet. Gold fell 2.82% and the dollar index firmed 0.31%, both standard responses to a hawkish repricing of the path of rates. For a levered balance sheet, the story today is not stock prices, it is that SOFR sits at 3.90% and the base rate just moved against you while the credit spread you pay on top of it got cheaper.

Macro Update

Waller's 50bp Warning Repriced the Curve, Not Just the Fed Funds Outlook

Fed governor Waller floated a half-point hike in response to hot inflation data while Cleveland Fed's Hammack argued for a gradual approach, and CNBC reports the Fed has gone from expecting cuts this year to bracing for a renewed inflation fight. The 10-year yield rose 2.05% to 4.98% and the 2-year rose 3.73% to 4.73%, a bear move across the curve that reflects the market pulling forward hike odds rather than any credit event. For leveraged borrowers, the relevant number is SOFR at 3.90%, which moves with this repricing immediately on floating rate exposure.

Read more

Waller's half-point hike comment, delivered against hot inflation prints, sits opposite Hammack's preference for a gradual approach, and CNBC's framing that the Fed is bracing for another inflation fight rather than delivering the cuts it had penciled in earlier this year is the headline that moved rates. The 10-year yield added 2.05% to reach 4.98%, within a hair of the psychologically loaded 5% level, while the 2-year added 3.73% to 4.73%, the front end moving harder than the long end as the market repriced near-term policy risk specifically.

This transmits to asset prices through the discount rate channel and, more directly for her seat, through the base rate on floating obligations. SOFR at 3.90% is the reference rate on most leveraged loans and revolvers, so a hawkish repricing raises the cash interest bill on existing floating rate debt without any change in the borrower's credit quality. The 2-year moving harder than the 10-year is the market expressing that the risk is a near-term hike, not a structural growth repricing, which is why it shows up as a bear-flattening rather than a bear-steepening move.

The notable second-order signal is that credit spreads did not widen with this. HY OAS tightened 1.20% to 3.30 and IG OAS tightened 1.10% to 0.90, meaning the entire yield move today was a base-rate story, not a default-risk story, and issuers are still pricing paper into spreads near the tights. That is exactly the backdrop in which Internet Brands is marketing a $3.5 billion debt sale for refinancing, a deal that makes more sense priced now, while spreads are cheap, than after a confirmed hike cycle pushes the base rate higher still.

The next hard data point is the December meeting itself and whatever CPI print lands ahead of it, since Waller's language was explicitly conditioned on inflation data. Watch whether Hammack's gradual camp gains votes or whether more regional presidents echo Waller, and watch HY OAS specifically, since a sustained break above the recent tights alongside further yield increases would be the first sign that the market is starting to treat this as a credit story rather than a pure rates story.

Sources: CNBC · EBC Financial Group · WSJ

A 1.31% S&P Print Hides a 1.64% Russell Decline

The S&P 500 rose 1.31% to 7,619.38 on the same day the Russell 2000 fell 1.64% to 278.44, the Dow fell 0.36%, and the Nasdaq fell 0.39%, a breadth split wide enough to matter. Adding a company-specific overhang, Commerce Secretary Lutnick has reportedly agreed to testify to the Senate Banking Committee following revelations tied to Nvidia's Jensen Huang, a headline risk sitting inside the index that is otherwise dragging. VIX falling 10.12% to 17.49 shows the options market is not treating the split as a volatility event yet.

Read more

The index-level numbers diverge sharply today: S&P 500 up 1.31%, Dow down 0.36%, Nasdaq down 0.39%, and Russell 2000 down 1.64%. Layered on top is the Nvidia scandal headline, with Lutnick agreeing to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on China chip sales tied to Jensen Huang, a specific, name-level overhang sitting inside a Nasdaq that is underperforming a cap-weighted S&P.

A capitalization-weighted index can rise on the strength of a small number of large constituents even as the median stock falls, which is the mechanical explanation for the S&P gaining while the small-cap Russell drops 1.64%. The Russell is the more useful read on the broader economy because its constituents are more domestically exposed, more likely to carry floating rate debt, and less able to absorb a higher SOFR without margin compression, so its underperformance alongside the 2-year yield's 3.73% jump to 4.73% is not a coincidence.

Smaller, more levered issuers are the segment most directly exposed to the base-rate repricing described above, since they refinance more frequently and carry less fixed-rate insulation. HYG's 0.48% decline is consistent with that read, a modest price give-back in high yield paper that lines up with a market nudging risk premia slightly higher in the credit-sensitive corner of the market even as index-level spreads stayed tight.

Watch the Senate Banking Committee testimony for headline risk to mega-cap tech specifically, and watch whether the Russell-to-S&P gap widens further, since a repeated pattern of narrow large-cap leadership against small-cap weakness is typically an earlier tell for credit stress among smaller issuers than anything visible in the index-level spread data.

Spreads Are Tight Into a Rising Refinancing Calendar

HY OAS tightened 1.20% to 3.30 and IG OAS tightened 1.10% to 0.90 on the same day Treasury yields climbed, signaling that today's higher all-in yields are almost entirely a base-rate phenomenon rather than a credit-quality repricing. Internet Brands is in the market with a $3.5 billion debt sale for refinancing, and PitchBook's 2027 European leveraged finance outlook and MacroMicro's note on AI-linked issuance against $40 trillion in US debt both point to a heavier supply calendar arriving into this window. The combination of tight spreads and rising supply is a technical that works until buyers start demanding more compensation.

Read more

HY OAS tightened 1.20% to 3.30 and IG OAS tightened 1.10% to 0.90 even as the 10-year yield rose to 4.98%, meaning spread compensation for default risk got cheaper on the same day the risk-free rate got more expensive. Against that backdrop, Internet Brands is marketing a $3.5 billion debt sale for refinancing, a deal sized to matter for the leveraged loan and high yield calendar, and PitchBook's outlook on 2027 European leveraged finance and MacroMicro's note on AI-linked issuance against a $40 trillion federal debt stock both flag a rising wall of supply arriving alongside it.

The mechanism is straightforward: when spreads are tight, the all-in coupon an issuer pays is driven almost entirely by the Treasury curve rather than by credit concerns, which is precisely why borrowers rush to price deals now rather than wait. Internet Brands pricing a refinancing into a 3.30 HY OAS environment is locking in the cheap half of the equation, the credit spread, even while the expensive half, the base rate, is moving against it in real time.

The risk sits on the demand side. A heavier issuance calendar, driven by refinancing walls and AI-linked capital needs against a national debt load north of $40 trillion, requires buyers to absorb more paper at spreads already near multi-year tights. HYG's 0.48% decline today is a small but visible sign that duration losses on existing holdings are outpacing the benefit of tighter spreads, which is the kind of mismatch that eventually forces spreads to widen to attract incremental buyers if issuance keeps accelerating.

The thing to watch is how the Internet Brands deal is priced and subscribed, since a well-oversubscribed book at tight spreads confirms the technical is intact, while a deal that needs to flex wider to clear would be the first concrete sign that the refinancing wall is starting to outrun demand. Also watch HY OAS specifically for any sustained move away from 3.30 if the 10-year pushes through 5%.

Sources: Bloomberg.com · PitchBook · MacroMicro
