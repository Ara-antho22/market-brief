Morning Edition

Friday, October 16, 2026

Newsletter Summary

Equities closed higher across the board, with the S&P 500 up 0.33% to 7,744.06, the Dow up 1.08%, and the Russell 2000 up 1.37%, even as producer prices posted their largest gain in four and a half years and CPI ran at 3.72% year over year. The VIX jumped 10.48% to 16.87, a reminder that the calm has a cost attached, and credit spreads moved with it: HY OAS widened 2.85% to 3.25 and IG OAS rose 1.20% to 0.84. The front end of the curve sold off, with UST2 up 0.63% to 4.82%, while the 10-year richened, yield down 0.38% to 5.19%, a flattening consistent with markets now debating whether the Fed's next move is a hike rather than a cut. CoreWeave remains the session's credit story, with its bonds yielding near 15% and JPMorgan reportedly trimming the size of a Qualtrics debt sale after banks got stuck holding over $5 billion of loans. Fed Governor Warsh talked up US resilience without giving any hint on the October decision, calling the AI buildout path 'very bumpy' but ultimately wealth-positive. None of today's individual moves are historic, but the combination of hotter inflation data, a hike-not-cut debate, and the first real stress test of AI-linked credit is the thing to carry into next week.

Macro Update

PPI's Biggest Jump in 4.5 Years Reopens the Hike Debate

Producer prices posted their largest gain since 2022, CPI is running at 3.72% year over year, and Reuters reports retail sales were boosted mainly by more expensive gasoline rather than underlying demand. The front end of the curve is pricing the shift: UST2 rose 0.63% to 4.82% while UST10 fell 0.38% to 5.19%, a flattening that reflects hike risk at the short end against a haven bid further out. Fed commentary has moved from 'when does the Fed cut' to whether it hikes in December if it skips October.

Read more

Producer prices rose at the fastest pace since 2022, the largest gain in roughly four and a half years according to Reuters and Bloomberg, while CPI sits at 3.72% year over year with the CPI index at 336.30, up 0.65% on the month. Retail sales came in firm, but Reuters attributes the strength to expensive gasoline rather than broad-based consumer demand. Unemployment ticked up 2.44% to 4.20%, so the data set is inflation running hot alongside a softening labor print, the combination that makes a Fed decision genuinely hard to call.

The transmission here runs through the front end of the curve rather than equities directly. Wholesale price pressure typically feeds into consumer prices with a lag, which is why SOFR, at 3.89% and up 0.26%, and UST2, up 0.63% to 4.82%, both moved to price less easing and some probability of a hike rather than a cut. That repricing raises the cost of carry on anything priced off short-term rates, which is the direct read-through to floating-rate leveraged loans and revolvers.

Spread markets are already reflecting the shift at the margin: HY OAS widened 2.85% to 3.25 and IG OAS widened 1.20% to 0.84 in the same session. For a leveraged finance desk, a hike-not-cut repricing combined with wider spreads means the all-in cost of new issuance moves higher on both the rate and the spread component simultaneously, which is the scenario that slows LBO financing and raises refinancing risk for issuers that assumed a declining rate path when they termed out debt.

Watch whether the Fed actually skips October, as Virginia Business reports some expect, with December now flagged as the more likely venue for any hike. Warsh gave no signal either way on the October meeting. The next PPI and CPI prints, and whether retail sales strength survives stripping out gasoline, are the specific data points that confirm or break the hike thesis.

Sources: Reuters · AP News · Virginia Business

CoreWeave Bonds Near 15% Test the AI Credit Chain

CoreWeave shares tumbled with its bonds yielding near 15%, and Reuters frames it as a spotlight on the debt burden behind the AI buildout. Separately, JPMorgan is reportedly weighing a smaller Qualtrics debt sale after banks were left holding over $5 billion in loans, a sign underwriters got stuck with paper the market did not want at the original terms. This is the first real test of whether AI-adjacent credit is priced for the leverage actually being used to build it.

Read more

CoreWeave's equity fell and its bonds now yield near 15%, a level CNBC and Reuters both frame as a market reassessment of the debt load tied to AI infrastructure buildout. Separately, JPMorgan is reportedly considering resizing a Qualtrics debt sale downward after banks ended up holding more than $5 billion in loans, meaning the underwriting syndicate could not place the paper at the terms originally assumed and is now sitting on inventory.

The mechanism runs from a single stressed name to the broader financing complex. When a bellwether AI infrastructure borrower sees its bonds reprice to near-distressed yields, every lender and underwriter with exposure to similar data-center and GPU-backed credits has to mark its own book against that new reference point, and banks holding hung loans either eat the mark-to-market loss or sell at a discount to clear it, which is exactly the dynamic behind the Qualtrics resizing.

That repricing is visible in the broader tape this session, with HY OAS up 2.85% to 3.25 and IG OAS up 1.20% to 0.84, both still tight in absolute historical terms but moving together. HYG was up only 0.42%, a smaller move than the OAS widening implies, which is a gap worth watching since cash credit ETFs sometimes lag what spreads are already pricing. For leveraged finance desks the open question is whether underwriters pull back on new AI-capex debt over the coming weeks or whether this stays contained to CoreWeave and a handful of adjacent names.

Watch whether CoreWeave's bond yield stabilizes near 15% or keeps climbing, and whether the Qualtrics deal actually gets resized or repriced wider when it comes to market. Reuters' own Morning Bid framed the question directly: calm after the CoreWeave shock, but for how long. That is the right lens for the next few sessions.

Sources: CNBC · Reuters · Quartz · Reuters Morning Bid

VIX Pops 10% Even as the Index Tape Stays Green

The VIX jumped 10.48% to 16.87 in a session where the S&P, Dow, Russell, Nikkei, and FTSE all finished higher, a divergence that points to protection demand concentrated in tech rather than a broad risk-off. Bloomberg notes Treasuries caught a haven bid as tech stocks slumped, consistent with the Nasdaq's much weaker 0.08% gain against the Dow's 1.08%. Warsh's refusal to signal on October, paired with his 'bumpy' AI framing, adds to the sense that the caution is specific to AI-linked names rather than the broader market.

Read more

The VIX rose 10.48% to 16.87 even as the S&P 500 added 0.33%, the Dow added 1.08%, the Russell 2000 added 1.37%, the Nikkei added 1.39%, the FTSE added 1.81%, and the Eurostoxx added 1.04%. The Nasdaq was the clear laggard at just 0.08%, underperforming every other major index listed. Fed's Warsh spoke publicly on US resilience, declined to hint at the October rate decision, and said the AI buildout path will be 'very bumpy' but ultimately good for wealth.

The Nasdaq's underperformance against the Dow and Russell, combined with the VIX pop, points to the protection bid being concentrated in mega-cap tech and AI-adjacent names rather than spread across the index. Bloomberg's framing, that Treasuries bounced as technology stocks slumped, matches the UST10 move, with yields down 0.38% to 5.19% even as UST2 rose, a pattern consistent with investors buying duration specifically as a hedge against tech-sector stress rather than a broad growth scare.

For credit, a rotation out of the richest AI-capex names into cyclicals and value is a healthier internal signal than an across-the-board selloff, but the simultaneous VIX pop and the spread widening in HY OAS and IG OAS this session mean options markets are paying up for protection before cash spreads have fully caught up. That gap tends to close one of two ways: either the VIX move fades once CoreWeave proves idiosyncratic, or cash credit spreads widen further to catch up with what volatility is already pricing.

Watch whether the VIX gives back today's gain or the Nasdaq catches down to match the protection bid, and watch for any explicit signal from Warsh or other Fed officials ahead of the October decision. Global equity funds have now drawn inflows for a fourth straight week per Reuters, a flow backdrop that argues against a durable risk-off unless credit spreads keep widening alongside the vol move.

Sources: Reuters · CNBC · Bloomberg.com · Fort Worth Star-Telegram

Equities fade as crude climbs

XLE fell 1.58% even as WTI rose 1.04% to $101.91, a rare split between crude and the equities that track it. The move in crude came from Russian strikes in Ukraine and renewed Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, both supply-threat headlines that would normally lift producer equities in tandem. Instead energy stocks sold off alongside a broader risk-off undercurrent, with the VIX up 10.48% to 16.87 and HY OAS widening 2.85% to 3.25%, even as the S&P and Dow posted gains elsewhere.

Crude's gain today is a geopolitical risk premium story layered on top of an IEA intervention story. Russian strikes in Ukraine and a fresh round of Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia are the proximate drivers pushing WTI to 101.91, but the IEA simultaneously announced it will speed up releases from strategic stocks, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel, a move explicitly aimed at capping the price impact of those same disruptions. The two forces are pulling in opposite directions on the forward curve even as spot grinds higher.

The equity reaction suggests the market is pricing the IEA's supply response more than the headline risk premium. XLE's 1.58% decline against a 1.04% rise in WTI is the kind of divergence that shows up when investors treat a geopolitical price spike as transient and discount it against an official body actively working to suppress it. There is nothing in today's headlines pointing to dispersion by subsector, integrated majors and E&Ps appear to be moving together on the downside, which is consistent with a broad derating of the sector's forward price assumptions rather than a stock-specific story.

The credit tape adds context for the equity move. HY OAS widened 2.85% to 3.25% and the VIX jumped 10.48% to 16.87 in the same session, a combination that typically raises the discount rate applied to cyclical, commodity-linked cash flows before any company-specific news. For leveraged E&P issuers, spread widening of this size on a single session is modest in absolute terms but still tightens the window for opportunistic refinancing if it persists, since high yield energy paper trades at a premium to the broader HY OAS print in most cycles.

The catalyst to watch is whether the IEA's release materializes in barrels on the tape fast enough to actually cap prices, or whether the Ukraine and Houthi-driven disruption risk proves more durable than the equity selloff implies. A escalation on either geopolitical front that disrupts actual flows, rather than just headlines, would force energy equities to re-couple with crude's move. Absent that, today's pattern argues the market is fading the spot spike.

Watch whether photonics and infrastructure names keep outrunning the index, since that would confirm the market is rotating within AI exposure rather than exiting it, and watch credit spreads for any follow-through beyond today's modest widening as the next tell on financing conditions for capex-heavy balance sheets.
