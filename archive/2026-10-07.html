Macro Update

10-Year at 5.31% as Global Tightening Wave Builds, Fed Minutes Due

The 10-year yield rose to 5.31% (+0.57%) as part of what Yahoo Finance describes as a global bond selloff driven by a 'collection of shocks,' with India's central bank hiking rates for the first time in nearly four years. Fed minutes due this week are expected to detail the central bank's own rate-hike decision and policy path. The matters because a rising risk-free rate repricing happens simultaneously across equity discount rates and credit spreads, not in isolation.

Read more

The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.31% on the session while the 2-year sits at 4.84% and SOFR at 3.90%. Reuters and Yahoo Finance both flagged a global bond selloff spreading across markets, with India's central bank delivering its first rate hike since 2023 and Reuters framing it as India joining a global rate-tightening wave. Separately, Reuters reports the Fed's own minutes are due and could detail the mechanics of its recent rate-hike decision and forward policy path, a notable framing given this is a hiking, not cutting, cycle.

The transmission mechanism here is straightforward but consequential: a higher 10-year yield raises the discount rate applied to future equity cash flows and simultaneously lifts the floor on floating-rate credit costs tied to SOFR. When multiple central banks tighten in parallel, as India's move signals, the move is not isolated Fed policy but a synchronized global repricing of the cost of capital, which compresses valuation multiples even as headline equity indices grind higher on idiosyncratic strength.

For financing markets specifically, the divergence between IG OAS, which tightened to 0.84 (-1.18%), and HY OAS, which widened to 3.12 (+0.65%), suggests high-grade issuers are still finding a receptive bid while lower-quality and floating-rate borrowers face a rising base rate layered on top of wider spreads. SOFR at 3.90% directly sets the coupon floor for leveraged loans, meaning any further base-rate increase compounds debt service pressure on existing floating-rate capital structures before a single spread basis point moves.

Watch the Fed minutes release for explicit language on how the committee is weighting the employment mandate against sticky inflation, and watch whether India's hike triggers follow-through tightening from other EM central banks, which would confirm this is a durable global repricing rather than a single-country event.

Sources: Reuters · Reuters · Yahoo Finance

Distressed Leveraged Loans Hit Pandemic-Era High, Tech in the Crosshairs

Deeply distressed US leveraged loans have climbed to their highest level since the pandemic, with tech names leading the deterioration according to JPMorgan. HY OAS widened to 3.12 (+0.65%) while HYG managed only a modest +0.38% gain, a divergence that matters directly for primary issuance and CLO book quality. This is the clearest credit-conditions signal in today's tape for anyone sitting on a leveraged finance desk.

Read more

Quartz, Briefs Finance, and Yahoo Finance Canada all report, citing JPMorgan, that deeply distressed US leveraged loans have risen to the highest level since the pandemic, with technology credits leading the increase. On the index side, HY OAS widened to 3.12 (+0.65% on the day) while the HYG ETF price only edged up 0.38%, a gap that signals the spread widening is concentrated rather than broad-based across the high-yield complex.

A loan is typically classified as distressed once it trades meaningfully below par, reflecting market-implied default or restructuring probability rather than a credit-rating action. Tech-sector leveraged borrowers are particularly exposed here because many carry elevated fixed capex commitments tied to data-center and AI infrastructure buildouts, while their loan coupons float off SOFR, currently at 3.90%, meaning debt service costs have already repriced higher mechanically even before any spread widening is applied.

For CLO managers, concentrated tech exposure in distressed paper raises the risk of mark-to-market losses and potential overcollateralization test breaches, which can trigger diversion of interest proceeds away from equity tranches. This also raises the bar for new leveraged loan issuance tied to tech sponsors, who will likely need wider original issue discounts or tighter covenant packages to clear the market, a dynamic that connects directly to the broader financing-arms-race question T. Rowe Price has raised around AI-related bond issuance pushing Treasury yields higher.

Watch the primary leveraged loan calendar for upcoming tech-sponsor deals and pricing talk, and watch for any CLO trustee reports flagging OC test breaches, which would confirm the distress is migrating from secondary mark-to-market into structural portfolio stress rather than remaining a trading-level phenomenon.

Sources: Quartz · Yahoo! Finance Canada · T. Rowe Price

Unemployment Rises to 4.20% as Payrolls Soften, Equities Shrug It Off

Unemployment climbed to 4.20% (+2.44%) and payroll growth is undershooting expectations, with one report citing just 29,000 jobs added, yet the S&P and Nasdaq are still pushing to records. The combination with CPI running at 3.35% year-over-year and a 5.31% 10-year is the uncomfortable mix: soft growth signals without the clean disinflation that would normally accompany them. That tension is what to watch heading into the Fed minutes.

Read more

Unemployment rose to 4.20% on the day's reading while CPI sits at an index level of 334.13 (+0.40%) and 3.35% year-over-year. Kitco reported job growth undershot expectations with the labor market described as merely 'stable,' LBM Journal cited a 29,000 jobs-added print, and CounterPunch flagged slowing job and wage growth broadly. Against that backdrop, equities kept climbing, with the S&P 500 up 0.58% and the Nasdaq up 0.45%, consistent with Investment Week's framing of AI-driven stocks hitting record highs while largely ignoring debt and growth concerns.

The mechanism that makes this combination unusual is that soft payrolls would typically argue for lower policy rates and falling yields, but ABC7 noted inflation had held steady before the Iran war sent gas prices surging, keeping headline inflation elevated even as labor demand cools. That keeps the Fed in a harder spot than a standard slowdown, with term premium staying elevated, as reflected in the 10-year at 5.31%, rather than the duration rally that typically accompanies weakening employment data.

For credit, a softening labor market raises default-rate assumptions for consumer-facing high-yield issuers just as debt service costs remain elevated with SOFR at 3.90%, a double pressure point for leveraged borrowers with exposure to discretionary spending. The VIX ticking up to 15.52 shows some pickup in hedging demand, but the level remains low in absolute terms, indicating the market has not yet moved to price a recession scenario despite the unemployment uptick.

The next confirming data point is the US jobless claims release flagged by investingLive as the test of whether weak payrolls are turning into actual layoffs rather than just slower hiring. If claims jump alongside the Fed minutes showing the committee still prioritizing inflation control, that combination would validate the stagflation-adjacent read rather than a benign soft landing.

Sources: Kitco · LBM Journal · ABC7 Los Angeles

Separately, Offshore Engineer flagged oil climbing on US storm threat and Saudi attack risk, and another wire described prices as merely stable on rising Middle East exports. Those are not fully reconcilable headlines on the same day, and that contradiction itself is a signal: the tape is trading two offsetting forces, a policy-driven supply release on one side and a persistent conflict-risk premium on the other, and neither has decisively won yet.

XLE's modest 0.47% gain against a 3%-plus crude decline is the dispersion to watch. 24/7 Wall St. notes refiner stocks are up over 160% even as crude slides, which is coherent if crack spreads are widening independent of the crude price level, diesel tightness from the same supply picture the G-7 is trying to address is a refiner tailwind, not a headwind. Natural gas names are also being bid as a Hormuz hedge, per OilPrice.com's list of gas stocks tied to the standoff, since any disruption to Gulf crude flows has historically pulled gas and LNG optionality along with it. Retail attention is reportedly concentrated in names tied to the prior run toward the $100 level, per Simply Wall Street, while separate coverage highlights E&P names screened specifically for strong cash flow exposure, a sign the market is rotating toward balance sheet quality rather than beta to the headline risk.

HY OAS widened to 3.12, up 0.65% on the day, while IG OAS tightened 1.18% to 0.84, a split that typically shows up when lower-quality, higher-cost-of-production credits lose pricing power faster than investment-grade issuers with diversified cash flow. For leveraged E&P names, a slide back toward the mid-90s on WTI, especially if it proves durable rather than a one-day reaction to the release headline, tightens the math on hedged production economics and raises the bar for refinancing non-investment-grade paper at attractive spreads. Refiners and gas-levered names with the cash flow profile highlighted by Yahoo Finance's screen are the more financeable credits in this tape, better placed to term out debt or fund buybacks without needing crude to cooperate.

The next hard catalyst is Wednesday's IEA meeting and whatever release size or timeline comes out of it. A large, coordinated diesel and crude release would reinforce today's move and likely pressure HY energy spreads further, while a smaller or delayed response, combined with any actual disruption through Hormuz, would snap the risk premium back into crude quickly and could reverse the refiner-over-upstream trade that's driven today's dispersion.

The dominant thread is still Nvidia-driven data center spending, but the character of the trade is shifting. Simply Wall Street and Stocktwits both frame the move as a broadening of AI infrastructure beneficiaries beyond the obvious chip names, with photonic and optical component makers COHR, AAOI, LITE, and POET catching a bid specifically on Google capex read-through. At the same time HPE is being asked whether it can sustain its AI infrastructure momentum through the capex cycle, and Microsoft's own coverage captures the tension directly: the company keeps winning in AI by the headlines' own framing, but the stock is described as still waiting, meaning the narrative has outrun the re-rating. Layered on top, BofA's note explicitly calls an end to the easy money phase of the AI-spending trade, a sentiment shift rather than a fundamentals call but one that matters for positioning.

The dispersion is the story. Optical and photonic names are a narrower, more levered-to-a-single-customer bet than the broad hyperscaler capex basket, which is why they are moving more sharply on a single Google data point than megacap platform names are. HPE sits in between, a legacy infrastructure name trying to prove it can hold AI-driven momentum rather than simply ride the initial re-rating. The financing backdrop is mixed in a way that is relevant to how this capex cycle gets funded. HY OAS, by contrast, rose to 3.12, up 0.65%, a small but directional move that suggests credit is starting to differentiate between balance-sheet-strong capex spenders and the more levered, smaller-cap suppliers feeding the photonics and optical rally. With UST10 at 5.31%, up another half point on the day, the discount rate applied to long-duration AI infrastructure payback keeps climbing, which raises the bar for any capex-heavy credit story that isn't backed by investment-grade cash flow, including vendor financing and SPV-style data center structures tied to the Nvidia supply chain.

From here the question is whether BofA's caution is a pause in positioning or the start of a real repricing. The next test is capex guidance commentary out of the hyperscalers themselves, since that is what the photonics and optical rally is directly levered to. A continuation of strong guides would likely keep funding the broadening trade despite the warning, while any softening in capex language, or further HY spread widening beyond the modest move seen today, would validate the end-of-easy-money call and push the dispersion between financed-by-IG megacaps and financed-by-equity-sentiment component suppliers even wider.

REITs rally while financing costs bite

XLRE rose 1.06% even as the 10-year yield pushed up to 5.31% and HY OAS widened five basis points to 3.12%, a divergence between the equity tape and the credit backdrop. The WSJ and Globest both flagged that the rate surge is forcing CRE buyers to revisit or unwind deals as loan maturities approach. Office remains the stress point, with Amazon subleasing more than half of its second Seaport tower, while Phoenix data showed both leasing activity and vacancy falling, a sign of shrinking footprints rather than healthy absorption. Frankfurt was the one bright spot, with prime office rent climbing to €55 on improving leasing momentum.

Read more

The dominant story in CRE right now is the collision between where deals were underwritten and where the cost of debt sits today. WSJ reports the rate surge is actively blowing up transactions, and Globest adds that buyers are being forced to revisit terms as loan maturities loom, a direct function of the 10-year at 5.31% (up 0.57% on the day) against SOFR at 3.90%. When debt yields on floating-rate loans reset against a front end this elevated, debt service coverage compresses fast, and sponsors who underwrote exits at lower cap rates are finding the spread between in-place debt cost and asset income no longer clears.

Office is where the dispersion is sharpest. Amazon's decision to sublease more than half of its second Seaport tower in Boston is a direct data point on gateway-market office demand destruction, adding sublease supply into a market still absorbing hybrid-work footprint cuts. Phoenix told a similar but more nuanced story: both leasing activity and vacancy fell together, which reads less like healthy absorption and more like tenants simply shrinking and transacting less, a stalled market rather than a recovering one. Frankfurt was the outlier, with prime rent rising to €55 on third-quarter leasing momentum, underscoring that this is a US-centric, rate-driven office problem more than a global one.

The financing angle is where the real signal sits. HY OAS widened to 3.12% (up 0.65% on the session) while IG OAS actually tightened to 0.84%, meaning the market is pricing dispersion by credit quality rather than a broad risk-off move. That matters directly for CRE capital structures: investment-grade-rated REITs retain reasonably priced access to unsecured debt, but the sponsors and operators financed through HY-rated or unrated structures, who are the ones facing the loan maturity wall Globest describes, are seeing their refinancing cost move the wrong way at the worst time. Floating-rate borrowers tied to SOFR at 3.90% and UST2 at 4.84% are squeezed from the front end while long-rate refinancing at 5.31% squeezes exit economics, pushing more of this bucket toward extend-and-pretend, mezzanine bridges, or forced equity paydowns rather than clean refinancing.

The setup from here is a tape that rallied on calmer macro conditions, a VIX at 15.52 and a broad market up across the board, rather than any actual repricing of CRE credit risk. That gap between the equity move and the fundamental headlines on deal breakage and loan maturities is the thing to watch. The catalyst that would close it is either a reversal in the 10-year back below levels that make refinancing math work again, or a fresh print on HY OAS that confirms spread widening is becoming a trend rather than a one-day move.

Sources: WSJ · Globest · The Business Journals · Your Valley · Cushman & Wakefield

US consumer holds, Europe retreats

Domestic data held up, with Fiserv's small business index showing September sales rising on back-to-school demand and Globest reporting consumer spending keeping shopping-center vacancy tight. That domestic resilience is running directly against a weakening European consumer, where eurozone retail sales missed forecasts again and UK retail sales fell 0.3%, intensifying calls for UK Budget support and raising questions over the ECB rate path. The sector rallied on the US side of that divide even as high yield spreads ticked wider.

Read more

The dominant story in discretionary today is a geographic bifurcation in consumer health. In the US, the Fiserv Small Business Index showed September sales rising as back-to-school spending lifted retail, and Globest's reporting on shopping-center vacancy points to physical retail demand still absorbing available space, a sign landlords and tenants tied to brick-and-mortar footprints are not seeing the demand erosion some feared. Across the Atlantic the picture is weaker: eurozone retail sales missed forecasts again per VT Markets and Action Forex, UK retail sales fell 0.3% with Fibre2Fashion noting growing calls for Budget support, and XTB flagged the data as a possible signal of falling fuel demand tied to weaker consumer activity. The divergence is wide enough that it is showing up directly in sector performance, with XLY's 1.18% gain roughly double the S&P's advance.

The dispersion is best read through subsector exposure to physical US footprint versus European consumer-linked revenue. Names with heavy brick-and-mortar US exposure are better positioned to benefit from the tight shopping-center vacancy dynamic Globest describes, since scarce available space supports both occupancy economics for retail landlords and pricing power for tenants that already hold leases. Conversely, any discretionary name with meaningful European revenue exposure is facing a demand backdrop that is deteriorating on two fronts at once, slowing retail sales and an ECB rate path now in question, which raises the risk of guidance softness from multinational retailers and apparel names with EU-denominated sales lines. TradingView's screen of discretionary names with A+ growth grades heading into Q4 suggests the market is already selectively rewarding names that look insulated from the European softness.

The financing backdrop is not fully confirming the equity move. HY OAS widened 0.65% to 3.12 even as IG OAS tightened 1.18% to 0.84, a divergence that typically reflects credit investors discriminating more carefully within lower-rated capital structures rather than a broad risk-off signal, since HYG itself was still up 0.38% on the session.
